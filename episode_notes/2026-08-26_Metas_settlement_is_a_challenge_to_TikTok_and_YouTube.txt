Meta’s settlement is a challenge to TikTok and YouTube

The cost of doing business as a global social media company just got a little higher.

Meta (META) agreed to settle a social media addiction case with a group of state attorneys general on Wednesday, marking a historic moment in the broader backlash against the rise and ubiquity of social media platforms.

Meta denied wrongdoing but is on the hook for $17 billion.

That figure, while a massive sum, is a fraction of the company's worth. More significant than the financial penalty is the precedent this agreement will set for seeking remedies for what many see as predatory app design and for how social media companies shape products moving forward.

New in-app rules to help parents and better protect children include a daily screen time limit, a default block during nighttime, and silenced notifications during school hours.

The agreement also has explicit provisions to encourage other social media companies to adopt the limits that Meta has agreed to.

Such industrywide adoption is the most significant and far-reaching consequence of the settlement.
