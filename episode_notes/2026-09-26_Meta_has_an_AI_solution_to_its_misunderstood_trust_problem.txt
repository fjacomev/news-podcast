Meta has an AI solution to its misunderstood trust problem

Meta's Muse has the "it" factor. After a flashy debut, it quickly won over early adopters and analysts for being the first capable AI agent for normies.

But a trickier question is whether Zuckerberg and company have the trust factor.

Of all the tech CEOs, perhaps none have as much trust and safety baggage as Zuckerberg. His rise to tech emperor was immortalized in the movie "The Social Network," which you could even say was about trust.

But will enough people feel assured enough in Meta to plug Muse into their wallets, calendars, inboxes, and shopping carts?

That's why, as Justin Patterson, KeyBanc managing director, told Yahoo Finance, "Meta is having its moment." After the Muse announcement, Patterson was among the analysts to raise his price target on Meta. He sees $900. Shares are up almost 13% over the past week.

Maybe saying users won't trust Muse to be their AI agent is denying how much people already depend on the Meta umbrella for their digital lives. Or maybe, as Meta sees it, people will take Muse's privacy safeguards seriously. Zuckerberg has emphasized that his teams designed state-of-the-art privacy and security into Muse's systems from the beginning.
