Parenting on the Fly? Same.

Confession: Looking back at the last few editions of this newsletter (the Snap "trial," the screen time requests I approved mid-meeting, the rules I created but never actually wrote down), I see a pattern. I'm not parenting with a plan. I'm parenting on the fly. Every call I make depends on the day, the kid, the friend group, and if I’m being honest, whether I'm really paying attention.

I have a feeling I'm not the only one. So this week I want to ask about

us

instead. Not "What's your kid doing that's driving you nuts?" (I know. I have two of those.) Instead:

What's standing in

your

way?

What keeps you from getting on track, or from even figuring out what "on track" looks like?

Maybe it's time to take a fresh look. Maybe you and your co-parent aren't on the same page, or you’re noticing that every other family seems to have different rules and you're the only "mean" one. Sometimes the technology just moves faster than you can keep up with. Or maybe it's something as simple, and oddly hard, as finding one movie your 7-year-old and your 14-year-old will both watch.

Screen Rules Are a Team Sport

My

daughters will argue with me for 10 minutes about putting the phone down, then hand it over to their grandmother without a word. Same kid, same phone, completely different reaction.

The hard part usually isn't the device itself. A lot of apps and games are designed without a natural stopping point, which means an adult has to become the stopping point. That handoff is hard. And kids often respond differently depending on who's asking.

Grandparents

: You often get a different version of the kid than parents do. Use that. Ask the parents what their rules are before the visit, then make your house the place where screens just aren't the main event. Have something ready to do instead or a project that needs their help. "I need an extra set of hands for this. Can you put that down and come help me?" works better than "That's enough screen time."

Aunts, Uncles, & Family Friends

: You get to be the fun adult, so use it. Give a heads-up instead of a hard stop ("Ten more minutes, then I want to show you something"), and follow through with something worth showing. Most importantly, back up the parents' rules, even when it'd be easier to be the cool one. Kids notice pretty quickly when one adult will bend a rule another one set.

Parents

: You have to do this every single day, which is probably why it's hardest. Give some warnings before you end ("After this episode" beats "Off. Now."), let them reach a natural stopping point when you can, and don't negotiate mid-meltdown. You can acknowledge that stopping is hard without changing the answer.

And yes, they're watching to see whether you can put your phone down too. (I'm talking to myself here.)

Trusted Adults (coaches, teachers, babysitters, mentors)

: Structure is your superpower. Set expectations early. Phones away at practice, devices off at dinner, whatever your rule is. Kids tend to handle a rule better when the rule is about the space, not about them. And if you explain why, older kids especially will meet you halfway.

Older Siblings:

You might have the most influence of anyone, and you don't even have to try. Don't play phone police; that just starts a fight. Instead, invite them into something: shooting hoops, a show you watch together, a walk to get a snack. Little siblings want to be where you are, doing what you're doing. If you put your phone down first, there's a good chance they'll follow.
