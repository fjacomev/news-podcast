How AI corporate travel is quietly lifting Delta

2026-10-11

Delta's (DAL) stock lost altitude on Friday morning due to a disappointing outlook, but investors can't blame the AI ecosystem for the shortfall.

Quite the contrary: The US's AI boom poured fuel on Delta's top and bottom lines.

Delta's corporate sales grew by double-digit percentages across all sectors, particularly those tied to AI, as execs from exposed industries likely traveled more to close deals, raise money, and visit data center sites.

"Corporate sales growth was led by the banking, technology, and energy sectors, with particularly strong performance in Boston and Los Angeles," Delta said.

Boston is a well-known financial services hub, while Los Angeles is a great landing spot for entering the tech ecosystem.

Despite the apparent AI-related momentum, Delta had a challenging third quarter amid surging fuel prices.

The Ed Bastian-led airline saw second quarter earnings land at $1.72 a share, below analyst forecasts for $1.82 a share. Delta called out a $500 million higher fuel bill compared to the guidance it offered in July.

Fuel expenses rose by $1.78 billion year over year, or 69%.

The company issued fourth quarter earnings per share guidance in a range of $1.15 to $1.65, well below consensus estimates. The Street was looking for $1.42.

Delta stock fell 1% in premarket trading.
