Microsoft reveals Maia 300 AI chip as it works to reduce dependence on Nvidia, AMD

2026-10-13

Por Daniel Howley

https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/microsoft-reveals-maia-300-ai-chip-as-it-works-to-reduce-dependence-on-nvidia-amd-130000212.html

Microsoft (MSFT) has debuted its latest AI chip, the Azure Maia 300, as it looks to reduce its reliance on third-party processors from the likes of Nvidia (NVDA) and AMD (AMD).

Unveiled at the OCP Global Summit 2026 in San Jose on Monday, the Maia 300 will join Microsoft's Maia 200 and Maia 100 chips as part of what the company said will be multigigawatt deployments beginning next year.

The Windows maker said that will make its Maia chips the "primary AI inference system for Microsoft products and services."

The company said Maia 300 provides 40% more tokens per megawatt than "leading GPU-based systems," a clear swipe at Nvidia, and that a 96-chip rack offers seven times the rack-scale token throughput of Microsoft's Maia 200.

Tokens are a basic unit of measurement in AI systems that can represent parts of words, images, and videos.

The more tokens per megawatt, the more efficient an AI rack.

The Maia 200 chip currently powers its Microsoft production workloads, including Microsoft 365 Copilot, as well as OpenAI (OPAI.PVT) models, Microsoft's MAI models, and open-source models. That provides 1.7 times more tokens per watt for AI capabilities in Microsoft's Teams, Copilot, and Cowork software versus other GPU-based systems, the company said.

Microsoft and fellow hyperscaler peers Amazon (AMZN), Google (GOOG, GOOGL), and Meta (META), as well as AI labs OpenAI and Anthropic (ANTH.PVT), either already offer or are working on their own custom chips.

Doing so acts as a hedge against becoming overdependent on processors from Nvidia and AMD. Producing their own semiconductors also allows companies to tailor them to their specific needs.

That's a potential problem for Nvidia, which regularly generates upwards of 50% of its quarterly revenue from hyperscalers.

On top of that, Amazon and Google now rent out their custom chips to third-party customers, a direct threat to Nvidia's business. What's more, Amazon is looking into selling chips for customers to install in their own data centers rather than running them from Amazon's own facilities.

That doesn't necessarily mean hyperscalers will stop purchasing Nvidia chips entirely. Customers still want access to a number of chips for different workloads, meaning the Amazons and Microsofts of the world will need to continue stocking up on the most in-demand processors.

And for now, those are Nvidia's.
