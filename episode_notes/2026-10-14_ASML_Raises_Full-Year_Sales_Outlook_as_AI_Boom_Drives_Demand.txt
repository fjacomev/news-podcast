ASML Raises Full-Year Sales Outlook as AI Boom Drives Demand

(Bloomberg) -- ASML Holding NV upgraded the low end of its 2026 sales forecast, the third consecutive quarter it raised its outlook, as soaring artificial intelligence spending boosts demand for the Dutch company's chip-making machines.

The Veldhoven, Netherlands-based firm projected annual sales of between €44 billion ($49 billion) and €45 billion this year, it said in a statement Wednesday. The low end of the range was previously €43 billion.

ASML is the only company that makes the cutting-edge lithography machines used to produce semiconductors for the data centers powering the AI boom. Its outlook offers an indication of investment plans by chipmakers like Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Samsung Electronics Co. and Intel Corp. as spending on AI infrastructure surges.

Industry players are increasingly questioning whether the AI boom is sustainable. There was a global selloff in semiconductor stocks this week after Broadcom Inc. Chief Executive Officer Hock Tan likened investor sentiment in the industry to a casino.

ASML's results can provide a window into chipmakers' plans because the company's advanced tools have a lead time of more than a year between order and delivery.

Net sales in the July to September quarter rose to €11.66 billion, ASML said. That compares with the average analyst estimate of €11.7 billion, according to data compiled by Bloomberg.

Investors are also looking for indications that ASML can fulfill orders while the market is hot. In July, ASML said it plans to boost capacity by 30% next year for its second-most advanced tool, the low NA extreme ultraviolet lithography machine, which would take its annual shipment target to about 85 units.

ASML said it would not fully meet demand in 2027 and 2028.

Some customers "are expressing an interest in placing orders with us for 2029 and even 2030," Chief Executive Officer Christophe Fouquet said in the statement. "We are investigating ways to increase capacity for 2028 beyond the 30% increase we previously communicated."

The company is working to ship tools faster to customers by reducing the cycle time, or the period between starting production in its clean rooms and shipping a completed machine. It began construction on its second industrial campus in the Netherlands, the first phase of which is set to be completed in 2029.
