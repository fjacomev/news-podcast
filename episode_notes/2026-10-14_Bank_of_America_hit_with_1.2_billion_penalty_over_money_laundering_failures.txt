Bank of America hit with $1.2 billion penalty over money laundering failures

2026-10-14

Por David Hollerith

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/bank-of-america-hit-with-12-billion-penalty-over-money-laundering-failures-201623796.html

Regulators fined Bank of America (BAC) more than $1.2 billion on Tuesday over widespread failures in the banking giant's safeguards against money laundering and other illicit financial activity.

Bank of America stock fell 1% during premarket hours.

The Treasury Department said the nation's second-largest bank failed to properly monitor trillions of dollars in transactions and to report more than $5 billion in suspicious activity, including transactions potentially tied to Chinese money-laundering networks, narcotics trafficking, and fraud.

The enforcement actions include a $1.2 billion penalty from the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), the third-largest fine in the Treasury Department's history after it fined crypto exchange Binance $3.4 billion in 2023 and TD Bank for $1.3 billion in 2024. The fine also includes a $750 million penalty from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), the largest ever imposed by that regulator.

The OCC penalty will be credited against FinCEN's assessment rather than added to it. Separately, a $11.6 million sanctions settlement brings the total penalties to more than $1.21 billion.

"Even the biggest financial institutions are not above the law," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a statement.

Bank of America's stock fell more than 2% after markets closed on Tuesday. It reports third quarter earnings Wednesday morning.

The bank said it will include a "non-tax-deductible charge of $1.2 billion impacting net income by approximately 17 cents per share" in its third quarter results.

"Financial crime compliance is serious, and we are responsible for getting it right," a Bank of America spokesperson said in an email statement, adding that the Charlotte, North Carolina based lender has invested "billions of dollars in technology" and dedicated "thousands of employees to combat financial crime" to resolve the deficiencies.

Regulators said Bank of America's failures extended across its transaction monitoring systems, customer due diligence, and internal oversight.

The bank failed to report more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions, including over $1 billion involving national security-related and other priority illicit finance risks.

FinCEN said Bank of America admitted to willfully violating the Bank Secrecy Act and had effectively outsourced substantial portions of its suspicious activity detection responsibilities to law enforcement and other financial institutions.

The bank also prioritized revenue over compliance resources, according to Treasury, allowing suspicious transactions to go uninvestigated for extended periods.
