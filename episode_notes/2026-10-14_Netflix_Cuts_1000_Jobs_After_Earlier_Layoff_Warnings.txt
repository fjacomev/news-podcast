Netflix is eliminating roughly 1,000 jobs worldwide, or about 6% of its workforce, in its largest round of layoffs since 2022,

The Wall Street Journal

reported Wednesday.

The cuts come as the streaming giant contends with slowing growth and concerns about subscriber engagement, while reassessing which areas of its expanding business are delivering results.

According to the Journal, Netflix co-CEOs Greg Peters and Ted Sarandos informed employees of the reductions in an email Wednesday morning. The bulk of the cuts will affect the company’s product, technology, legal and human resources teams, according to the report.

The Journal’s report follows earlier coverage from my Too Much TV Newsletter,

which first reported on Oct. 1

that Netflix was preparing for a major round of layoffs that could affect as many as 1,000 employees, or approximately 5% of its workforce. I reported that the cuts reflected both slowing engagement and pressure on the company’s stock, as well as an effort to shift resources away from underperforming divisions and areas executives viewed as having limited revenue potential.

Netflix’s push into physical experiences has been one area of particular scrutiny. Greg Lombardo, who led the company’s retail efforts, including its Netflix House attractions in Philadelphia and Dallas, is leaving after seven years. His departure followed the earlier exit of Pam Levine, the company’s former vice president of marketing and publicity for the U.S. and Canada.

I reported that sources inside Netflix viewed the retail and experiential business as promising but said the company had not yet figured out how to make the initiatives justify their costs through engagement and other returns.

Gaming also remains an open question. Netflix has spent heavily on its games business but has struggled to establish a meaningful position in the competitive market. The company closed its Hollywood-based Night School Studio in August and also announced plans to close its Helsinki-based Moonloot studio. Ellis reported that further changes in the division were possible.

The restructuring comes as Netflix faces increased

scrutiny over engagement and competition for viewers’ time

In a recent interview at Bloomberg’s Screentime conference,

Sarandos acknowledged that Netflix was not growing as quickly as he wanted

. The company has continued to pursue new programming and distribution opportunities even as it looks to sharpen spending and prioritize initiatives that can generate stronger returns.

The layoffs also recall Netflix’s 2022 restructuring, when the company cut hundreds of jobs amid a difficult period for the streaming business.

I noted in my initial report

that some executives view those earlier cuts as a precedent for a broader strategic reset, rather than simply a cost-reduction exercise.

For Netflix, the latest reductions represent an effort to recalibrate the company after years of expansion into adjacent businesses. The precise impact across individual divisions remains to be fully established, but the combination of workforce reductions and leadership departures suggests that the company is taking a harder look at where it invests and how it measures success.
