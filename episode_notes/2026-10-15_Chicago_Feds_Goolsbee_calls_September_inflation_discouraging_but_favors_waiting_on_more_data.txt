Chicago Fed's Goolsbee calls September inflation 'discouraging' but favors waiting on more data

2026-10-15

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/chicago-feds-goolsbee-calls-september-inflation-discouraging-but-favors-waiting-on-more-data-185843740.html

Chicago Federal Reserve president Austan Goolsbee called a hotter-than-expected reading on September inflation "discouraging" but suggested that he wants to wait for more data before taking action to raise interest rates higher.

"We need to get more data," Goolsbee said in an interview with Yahoo Finance. "We need to get the through line. If the through line on inflation is this 4% core inflation, then that's not acceptable. That's not compatible with the Fed target, and our policy needs to address that."

That 4%, he said, refers to a projection of September's core price change over a full 12-month period, showing what the annual inflation rate would be if that exact monthly pace kept up for an entire year. The Consumer Price Index rose 3.7% in September, compared with expectations for 3.6%, and marking an acceleration from 3.4% in August.

Stripping out volatile food and energy prices — the way the Fed likes to scrutinize underlying inflation — CPI rose 0.3% month over month, compared with expectations for 0.2%, holding the same level as August. The year-over-year rate was 2.6%, compared with expectations for 2.5%, and up from 2.4% in August.

"I found it discouraging, and to pile on, last month was also discouraging," Goolsbee said.

He's concerned not just about the overall number but also about services inflation, which he sees showing little sign of improvement, with a reading above 5%. He noted that higher services inflation can't be chalked up to tariffs or higher oil prices. Core goods inflation above 3% should have come down as tariffs made their way through, Goolsbee said, but it hasn't, pointing toward more persistent inflation. He also noted that the AI data center build-out warrants close watch because of its potential to overheat the economy.

On the ground in his Midwestern district, Goolsbee said he's hearing all about costs and prices.

"There's very little that we're hearing that sounds like downturn, risks of recession, and things like that," he said. "So that makes you more nervous about demand side overheating than just straight supply shock."

Asked whether rates are high enough at current levels to bring inflation back down to 2% or whether additional rate hikes are needed, Goolsbee said, "I don't think we yet know the answer to that."

He added, "If we get more months like this … that's totally unacceptable."

Goolsbee noted that he's one of the more "optimistic folks" at the Fed about how much progress can be made on inflation over the next year — but added the caveat that he said in the fall of 2024 that he was uncomfortable with front-loading rate cuts on the premise that inflation would be transitory.
