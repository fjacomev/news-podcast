Netflix stock sinks after announcing Q3 earnings, Q4 revenue forecast falls short of estimates

2026-10-21

Por Ines Ferré

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/netflix-stock-sinks-after-announcing-q3-earnings-q4-revenue-forecast-falls-short-of-estimates-204545261.html

Netflix (NFLX) stock fell 6% in premarket trading on Wednesday as the streaming giant's Q4 revenue outlook came in below Wall Street estimates, signaling slower growth.

The company posted revenue of $12.87 billion in its fiscal third quarter, in line with analyst estimates. Earnings per share came in at $0.87, topping estimates of $0.82.

Fourth-quarter outlook for the company's top line, though, came in at $13.44 billion, short of the consensus analyst estimate of $13.51 billion.

Netflix also cut its 2026 ad revenue guidance from $3 billion to $2.7 billion due to a slower-than-expected rollout of certain ad product capabilities.

Wall Street has been laser-focused on engagement, which management argued was healthy given the response to this year's price increases.

When it comes to the US, "our engagement is solid, and it will allow us to continue to grow as we have for the last several years," Greg Peters, co-CEO of Netflix, said during the earnings call.

"But we know that we can do better…It's our goal to go from very good to great," he added.

Wall Street analysts were looking for signs on whether slowing engagement would bleed into the revenue growth numbers.

"That is exactly what we are seeing with the 4Q growth outlook," Geetha Ranganathan, senior media analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence, told Yahoo Finance after the results were released.

"This kind of speaks to a slowing growth algorithm, which kind of spells trouble for the company without a doubt," she added.

The results follow the streaming giant's job-cutting plan and a leadership shake-up announced last week amid slowing viewer engagement.

Last week, former Disney executive Peter Rice was named Netflix's next chief content officer. Rice replaces Bela Bajaria, who worked at the streamer for nearly a decade and served as CCO since 2023.

Also last week, Netflix said it would cut 6% of its workforce, or about 1,000 employees, marking the company's largest reduction of employees since 2022.

The move comes as the company seeks to reinvigorate viewership growth and strengthen its content amid growing competition and short-form viewing habits.

Audiences have increasingly moved away from traditional television sets toward mobile devices, noted Piper Sandler director and senior research analyst Tom Champion in a recent interview.
