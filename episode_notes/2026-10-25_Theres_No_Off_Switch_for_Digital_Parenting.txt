The Second Shift That Is Digital Parenting

Feel free to read this part aloud to your partner, co-parent, or any other supporting caregiver in your family's life. ;) Seriously. As if the usual responsibilities of being an adult

and

a parent aren't enough, digital parenting comes with its own running list: Parental controls. Decisions. Updates. Dashboards. Finding the good apps. Blocking the bad content. Keeping up with the workarounds. Plus the quiet dread of constantly wondering whether you've missed something.

And that's just the devices.

This is why I'm always telling parents, "Give me a scary movie any day." A scary movie has been scripted, edited, and produced by professionals. I know what's in it. I can read a review. It's everything I

can't

preview that worries me: the posts, the comments, the texts, the things my kids come across when I'm not there, and whether those things are lodging somewhere in their mind. Sometimes I wonder what they've seen that they were too scared, too embarrassed, or simply too used to seeing to think they should tell me about it.

The Digital Mental Load is all too real.

If you're sharing the parenting, you should be sharing this part, too.

Learn the platforms. You don't need to know every feature. But know how your kids are using them, where the settings live, and enough to follow the conversation when something comes up.

Take the lead on some of the hard stuff. Own part of the setup, the monitoring, or the "no, not that app" conversations, so the person who usually handles it gets to enjoy some of the fun. We want to pick the movie and share the funny videos with the kids too!

Share the parental controls. Running this space can feel like a second job. If one person always knows the passwords, settings, rules, and latest workaround, that person is probably carrying too much of it. Split up what you can.

Think about one part of the digital load you could hand off, share, or ask another caregiver to take on this month. Here are some

tips for setting tech boundaries with your children's caregivers

Maybe your toddler is stuck on a loop of one show. Maybe your 7-year-old is suddenly watching a teen creator aimed at much older kids. Whatever the reason, Netflix

does

let you block specific titles. The annoying part: You have to do it one by one.

The fix, for now: Block specific shows

Go to

Account

, then

Profiles

, and choose your child's profile.

Open

Viewing restrictions

. Netflix will ask for your account password.

Under

Title restrictions

, type each show's name, and select it to block it.

Save.

The blocked shows disappear from that profile, though it can take a little while to update on the TV.

A few other tips to help for now:

Lower the maturity rating.

Some creator shows aimed at older kids may disappear automatically if the profile is set to a younger rating.

Lock the other profiles.

Add a PIN to adult profiles so kids can't easily switch over.

Check back occasionally.

Netflix is adding more creator content, so a title-by-title block list may need updating.

Why "for now"? Because this is a manual, title-by-title fix for what's clearly a growing strategy. Every new creator deal means another name to add. It works, but it puts the work on us.

best kids' shows on Netflix.

One More Thing

Thank you for your support, and for receiving what honestly felt like a complete and total disaster with so much kindness. My daughter has been really receptive to what I'm calling her "makeunder." We've been talking about where she wants to put her time and energy. A lot of it goes to soccer, but there's room for friends and downtime too. Without overstructuring it, we've been talking about steering toward things that make her feel good, or at least better, about herself, and away from the things that leave her questioning herself and her decisions.

And when she does make those choices anyway (because teens, amirite?), we're talking about how to learn from them without being too hard on herself. We've been chatting about the difference between age-appropriate boundary pushing and the kind of thing that pressures her to grow up before she's ready. It's not a neat ending. But it feels like a better beginning.

Warmly,

Jill
