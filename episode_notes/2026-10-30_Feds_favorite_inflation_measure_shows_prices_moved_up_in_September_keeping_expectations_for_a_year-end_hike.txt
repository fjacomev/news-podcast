Fed’s favorite inflation measure shows prices moved up in September, keeping expectations for a year-end hike

2026-10-30

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/news/pce-inflation-prices-ticked-higher-in-september-keeping-expectations-for-a-year-end-fed-hike-143025383.html

A fresh reading of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge showed prices heated up in September, solidifying expectations for an interest rate hike in December.

The Personal Consumption Expenditures index rose 3.6%, in line with expectations and up from 3.4% a month earlier. On a core basis, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, PCE rose 3.1%, also in line with expectations and up a tenth of a percentage point from 3% in August. Month over month, core PCE clocked in at 0.3%, in line with projections, but marking a faster pace from the 0.2% seen in August.

"September PCE reaffirmed that the price side of the Fed's congressional mandate is where they have their work cut out," said Ariane Curtis, senior economist for Capital Economics, noting that the breadth of price pressures broadened with core services excluding housing rising nearly 0.4%.

Thursday's data confirmed that "the more encouraging inflation data over the summer was not the start of a new trend," Curtis added.

The hotter data was anticipated, prompting many analysts to feel a rate hike was in order Wednesday, given other inflation readings ahead of the Fed's meeting that showed price increases last month.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh provided no detailed explanation Wednesday for why the central bank chose to hold rates steady after a hot reading on the Consumer Price Index and ahead of the expected rise in core PCE. Heating inflation was the catalyst for September's hike.

"Today's data strengthens the case for another rate increase in December," said Chris Osmond, chief investment officer for Fifth Third Wealth Advisors. "Economic growth remains above trend, core inflation is still above target and headline inflation has reaccelerated. Yesterday's hold increasingly looks like a pause rather than the end of the tightening cycle."

Markets are pricing in a better-than-80% chance that the central bank hikes rates in December.

While Warsh didn't send an explicit signal that the Fed would hike at its next meeting, he did say that since September credit conditions have tightened for some businesses and households but still aren't tight, implying there's room for the central bank to raise rates further.

"Since our policy action in September, we have started to see some changes in financial and credit conditions," Warsh said. "They have tightened for some businesses and households, much as we would expect, market participants are becoming somewhat more discerning in their capital allocations. And while overall financial conditions are tighter, I'm unwilling and unable to call them tight."
