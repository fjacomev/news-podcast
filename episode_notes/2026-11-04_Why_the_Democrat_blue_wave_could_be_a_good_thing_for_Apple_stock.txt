Why the Democrat blue wave could be a good thing for Apple stock

2026-11-04

Por Brian Sozzi

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/why-the-democrat-blue-wave-could-be-a-good-thing-for-apple-stock-100051134.html

True to form, Wall Street is wasting no time picking winners and losers from the fresh Democratic blue wave that washed through the midterm elections late Wednesday.

One winner could be Apple for two reasons, opines EvercoreISI analyst Amit Daryanani.

The first is more of a defensive play amid the unknown that is fresh DC gridlock.

"Apple offers a different earnings driver through iPhone mix/pricing and services rather than data-center construction; our work points to resilient demand, with gross-margin recovery and price elasticity the key tests. This is THE name to keep working when investors want to get defensive," Daryanani says.

The other aspect is cooling memory chip prices, a major headwind for Apple, as data center projects get delayed or outright blocked by the Dems.

Said Daryanani, "Memory input inflation (DRAM & NAND) has been a sizable headwind for a host of names in our coverage (AAPL for example, talked about 140bps GM headwind in Dec-qtr due to memory, but also PC names). Could a slowdown in DC buildouts actually temper memory demand and, in a way, help companies that have been impacted by the sizable memory inflation (AAPL is the key one but there are many others)?"

Apple shares rose 0.5% in pre-market trading on Thursday.

The midterm election results threaten to significantly disrupt AI data center expansion as voter backlash over soaring utility bills, grid instability, and water usage drives a sharp political pivot toward tighter regulatory oversight.

In Texas, a dramatic political shift saw James Talarico capture a US Senate seat and Gina Hinojosa oust Greg Abbott in the gubernatorial race. This brings unprecedented scrutiny to ERCOT's independent grid allocations and unchecked tech consumption.

Paired with Senate wins by Roy Cooper in North Carolina and Sherrod Brown in Ohio, along with Amy Acton's win for governor there, these victories signal that the era of friction-free, subsidized hyperscaler build-outs could come to an abrupt end.

"Ohio, Texas and Georgia and that has implications for the AI trade and data center buildout. In the end – voters delivered a clear warning to the AI buildout: the money may be available, but local permission to build is becoming harder to secure and project delays can shift the timing of the spending but it does NOT eliminate the demand," said Slatestone Wealth chief market strategist Kenny Polcari.
