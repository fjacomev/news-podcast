Democrats' victories in Texas laid bare voter unease over data centers — and signal new hurdles for the industry

A strong showing by Democrats in Texas swept Gina Hinojosa into office as the state's first Democratic governor since 1990 in a contest closely watched by Big Tech companies.

Texas is considered a bellwether for government restrictions on AI data centers and was just one place where deep voter unease over the build-out was on display. Voters across the country passed a series of local ballot measures aimed at restricting construction in the years ahead.

Hinojosa, Texas's first Latina governor, has pledged to impose a moratorium on data center construction in Texas, which accounts for more than 25% of planned US capacity by 2030.

"I want to say something to the powerful people who have gotten very comfortable running this state," she told supporters in her victory speech. "The voters have delivered a mandate for change, and it is change they will get."

Her victory was one of a series of wins for Democrats in Texas. The party is also on track to take control of the Texas House, which would give Hinojosa more power to follow through on restrictions even as the state Senate appears set to remain under Republican control.

"We view the Texas gubernatorial race as particularly important for the next leg of the AI trade," wrote Raymond James's Ed Mills in a pre-election note saying that a strong Democratic showing there "could signal a broader electoral wave while increasing concerns about data center moratoriums and more restrictive AI regulation in the nation's largest planned data center development."

Bank of America called the race "one of the highest impact elections for the entire utility sector."

A race that 'could make a real difference'

Hinojosa made opposition to data center construction a central part of her campaign, charging in one ad that Gov. Greg Abbott "works for" Elon Musk.

She staked out plans for a moratorium on new data center construction, stricter regulations on water and power usage, and an end to tax breaks.

Abbott was seeking his fourth term as governor and said last year he wanted Texas to be the "epicenter of AI development."

More recently, he announced a moratorium of his own and said he would support more stringent requirements on power and water usage. Hinojosa immediately charged that Abbott's new proposals amounted to an "election year pause."

Tobin Marcus of Wolfe Research said Texas "is the one state with enough scale and enough difference between the candidates that it could make a real difference."
