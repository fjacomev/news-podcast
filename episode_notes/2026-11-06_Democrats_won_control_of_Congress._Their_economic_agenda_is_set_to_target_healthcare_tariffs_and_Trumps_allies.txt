Democrats won control of Congress. Their economic agenda is set to target healthcare, tariffs, and Trump's allies.

Democrats retook power in Washington on Tuesday, flipping key House and Senate seats to command majorities in both chambers of Congress.

The power shift creates a new platform for Democrats to launch investigations of President Trump's economic allies — Elon Musk, to name one — and pass bills that may be unlikely to become law but will mark a sharp change in the political climate for business leaders and economic issues after two years of unified Republican control.

The change also appears set to elevate figures like Democratic Sen. Elizabeth Warren and others to prominent positions with oversight over economic policy.

Democrats are planning "a shock-and-awe legislative sprint," the New York Times reported, on issues like healthcare subsidies and Trump's tariffs, which could prompt a wave of bills to pass the House but stall against the 60-vote threshold in the Senate. Even in the minority, Senate rules mean Republicans retain the power to stop legislation.

Democrats are also likely to seek areas of bipartisan agreement on pharmaceuticals, the housing market, and AI data centers, all sectors that both Republicans and Democrats have spotlighted on the campaign trail.

Divided government, of course, may also set the stage for political clashes that could destabilize markets and the broader economy.

"While conventional wisdom suggests equities tend to rally following midterm elections, particularly when the outcome produces divided government, we caution that the post-election environment may look considerably different this cycle," Raymond James Washington Policy Analyst Ed Mills warned in a pre-election note.

He added that much of the recent volatility has been driven by unexpected moves from the White House. "We believe Trump will increasingly test the limits of executive authority during the remainder of his term," Mills wrote.

'Lawyer up': An expected focus on Elon Musk and Trump's economic team

Going by early signals, Democrats intend to aim their new powers at an array of high-profile targets, with Musk chief among them, as part of a far-reaching effort to impose new checks on the Trump administration.

"Lawyer up," said Rep. Robert Garcia, who is poised to become chair of the House Oversight Committee, in one particularly pithy signal of the Democrats' plans.

Recent developments involving Elon Musk and Starlink in Ukraine are just the latest topic that Democrats say they will investigate around the world's richest man. The Wall Street Journal has reported that Musk ordered the slowdown of a military-grade version of Starlink relied on by Ukraine and discussed the decision on a call with Trump in October.

A group of Silicon Valley lawmakers said in a letter the issue raises broader concerns and could lead them to "closely scrutinize federal funding awarded to Mr. Musk's businesses" in the next Congress.

Musk could also face questions about his role in the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) and SpaceX's other government contracts.

Trump this week said he would consider preemptively pardoning officials like Musk who face legal scrutiny from Democrats, saying in an interview, "I would if I needed to, sure. I wouldn't even think about it."

Capitol Hill observers are also expecting economic-themed clashes, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent potentially facing new scrutiny.

Politico recently reported that Democrats are planning to investigate the Treasury Department and the IRS, as well as Trump's actions to rein in the power of financial regulatory agencies.

Bessent's clashes with Democratic lawmakers have already been caustic and highly personal over the last two years, and that could only intensify as Democrats gain the power to subpoena documents and compel testimony, with Sen. Warren likely to lead the Senate's influential Banking Committee.

This new subpoena power could also put an array of executives in the hot seat, from tech executives facing potential hearings over harmful AI-agent behavior to CEOs being called to answer allegations of price gouging across sectors.

Sectors where 'investors might see a glimmer of hope'

The changeover in power has implications — good and bad — for numerous business sectors.

Democrats could seek to boost sectors like clean energy by blocking further efforts by Trump to cut tax credits.

Stifel Chief Washington Policy Strategist Brian Gardner offered clean energy as the top potential winner in his final midterm election preview.

"Investors might see a glimmer of hope that clean energy tax credits could be revived or extended," he wrote this week, adding that the credits "also have some GOP support so they could be revived even in a split Congress."

Rep. Brendan Boyle, who is set to be the next chairman of the House Budget Committee, said in a statement, "I will fight to reverse his costly tariffs and devastating healthcare cuts, and bring an end to the war in Iran."

Moves to reverse Trump's tariffs are almost sure to be met with a presidential veto, but more bipartisan areas could sidestep the president.

The housing issue earlier this year delivered a potential proof of concept when a major piece of legislation passed with bipartisan majorities and became law despite Trump's refusal to sign it.

During the now-concluded campaign season, candidates from both parties focused criticisms on pharmaceuticals and AI data centers, a sign that these sectors may be ripe for bipartisan compromise.
