Fed’s Barkin Says Policy Direction Clear, But Needs Careful Pace

2026-11-10

Por Enda Curran; Aidan Williams

(Bloomberg) -- Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond President Tom Barkin signaled robust demand and stubbornly high inflation mean interest rates will need to go higher, though he cautioned that any hikes will need to be done at a careful pace.

In a speech outlining his overview of the economy, Barkin described corporate profits as remarkable and noted that consumers continue to spend in an economy experiencing a record streak of unemployment at or below 4.5%.

But he cautioned on how long consumer strength can last and highlighted that both businesses and households continue to complain about rising costs, making the path but not the pace clear for policymakers.Rich

"The appropriate direction of monetary policy is relatively straightforward," Barkin said in a speech in South Carolina. "However, risks to the demand outlook remain easy to envision. Because of that, it is wise to move policy with care."

Fed officials held interest rates steady last month, saying the "accumulation of more information" would allow them to determine the best level for policy. The 10-2 decision to stay put followed a unanimous move to lift the benchmark rate in September by a quarter percentage point to a target range of 3.75% to 4%.

Several policymakers speaking since the meeting have touted the need for additional rate hikes, and traders see about an 80% chance of a hike in December.

Data last week showed jobs growth surged in October. and the unemployment rate edged lower. Fed officials will see one more jobs report and a full slate of inflation data — beginning with the consumer price index for October on Tuesday — before they gather again in December.

Barkin described the AI boom as the "elephant in the room" for policymakers. How it plays out will be especially consequential for the economy in the near term, he said.

"If it underdelivers, business investment could pull back, stock prices could follow, and so too could asset-fueled consumer spending," Barkin said. "The risk runs the other way, too. AI could overdeliver and displace more workers than expected, resulting in a pullback in spending. Either scenario would move attention to the maximum employment part of our mandate."
