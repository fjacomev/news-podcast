Morgan Stanley buys Vinyl Equity to capture more IPO wealth

2026-11-10

Por David Hollerith

Morgan Stanley (MS) said Monday it has agreed to acquire Vinyl Equity, a company that serves a lesser-known back-office function, as the Wall Street giant looks to deepen its services around taking companies public.

The acquisition would give Morgan Stanley greater control over the transfer of shares from private companies into public markets, a move that bolsters its workplace wealth management business, which administers employee stock plans for millions of workers across corporate America.

Terms of the deal were not disclosed. The transaction is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027.

Morgan Stanley already offers administration services to more than 1,000 private companies, representing nearly 70% of existing unicorns, according to Scott Whatley, head of Morgan Stanley at Work. Those companies will be the initial focus for the newly acquired business.

The potential payoff from those relationships has become increasingly evident in Morgan Stanley's recent earnings.

In its second and third quarters, the bank's IPO-related stock flows accounted for more than half of the $380 billion in net new assets attracted by the bank's wealth management division, according to company executives.

Speaking at a capital markets conference on Monday afternoon, Morgan Stanley wealth management chief Jed Finn said transferring shares from private to public ownership can take as long as two weeks through traditional transfer agents, while Vinyl's technology can complete the process on a next-day settlement timeline.

Finn said corporate clients had sought the capability for years, partly because they wanted to avoid transferring sensitive shareholder information among multiple outside firms.

"We're finding ways to be easier to do business with for our clients," Whatley said, adding that removing third-party transfer agents "will increase the probability" of retaining assets and converting them into fee-based flows.

The acquisition is the latest move by Morgan Stanley to deepen its relationships with private companies and their employees before they go public, following its acquisition of private-share marketplace EquityZen earlier this year. (Disclosure: Yahoo Finance has a partnership with Forge Global and EquityZen.)

Whatley said bringing share transfers within Morgan Stanley could also help the bank retain more client assets and convert them into fee-generating wealth-management relationships.

The deal could also position Morgan Stanley to capitalize on the emerging market for tokenized securities, which use blockchain technology to record and transfer ownership of financial assets.
