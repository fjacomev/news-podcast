Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's debacle highlights this one glaring issue

2026-11-10

The Nvidia (NVDA) story has been led by Jensen Huang since the company's founding in 1993, sketched out on a napkin at a Denny's.

Huang's passion for computing chips (first gaming chips, now AI chips) was on display in an endless stream of interviews, in his signing autographs for enthusiastic fans on his world travels, and in his fried chicken dinner with fellow tech titans overseas that went viral.

His star power was evident in the curiosity among the crowds at the annual GTC event about which black leather jacket he would wear on stage to pump up the future of AI.

The market's infatuation with Nvidia has gone hand in hand with its infatuation with Huang and the technology he pioneered, which helped create one of the world's most valuable companies.

Huang is like Nvidia's sun — the center around which the whole show runs.

But a fresh scandal involving the most visible CEO in the world has raised a valid question among investors, perhaps for the first time ever.

What in the world is Nvidia's succession plan? Does one even exist?

"Every high-profile company carries key-person risk, and Nvidia is no exception," FedWatch strategist Ben Emons said. "While Jensen Huang's full cooperation suggests, in my view, that he has little reason to conceal information and likely did not share sensitive data with the alleged spy, the initial cover-up and later disclosures will still likely weigh on investor confidence."

Nvidia's board of directors has launched an independent investigation into reports that a woman CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with was allegedly spying on him for the Chinese government.

Huang admitted to the affair on Sunday in an email to employees.

Nvidia shares fell 5% in early trading on Monday as a dark cloud settled over Nvidia and its once-Teflon CEO's future.

Huang has never talked about a succession plan publicly. Nor is anything of the sort disclosed in public filings such as proxy statements or the annual report. The only two people on Nvidia's earnings calls are Huang and longtime CFO Colette Kress.

The top four leaders on Nvidia's investor relations page all hold the title of executive vice president: Colette Kress, Nick Parker, Debora Shoquist, and Tim Teter. Usually at these levels of leadership, someone being groomed as a CEO successor would also carry the president title alongside their formal title, such as CFO or COO.

By all outward indications, Huang has displayed the typical behavior for an ultra-wealthy founder CEO — one that thinks they'll be doing the job forever and won't slow down at all. For these folks, succession planning is usually far down the totem pole.
