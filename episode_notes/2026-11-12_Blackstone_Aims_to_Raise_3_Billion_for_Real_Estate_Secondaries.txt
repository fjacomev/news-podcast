Blackstone Aims to Raise $3 Billion for Real Estate Secondaries

(Bloomberg) -- Blackstone Inc. is pitching a fund that will snap up stakes in other asset managers' real estate vehicles, expecting to raise at least $3 billion amid a prolonged need for liquidity in the sector.

Strategic Partners, the firm's secondary business, raised $2.6 billion for a prior iteration of the fund in 2023, with executives citing a growing demand for cash among both money managers and their investors, known as limited partners.

The latest fund aims to acquire interests in core real estate funds from asset managers and their clients, at a discount to the net asset value, according to people familiar with the matter. It will look to invest primarily in funds with data center, rental housing and industrial assets, the people said, requesting not to be identified because the fundraising isn't public.

A representative for Blackstone declined to comment.

The US real estate market has been hammered in recent years, with the Covid-19 pandemic hitting offices particularly hard. The Federal Reserve's rate increases starting in 2022 further curbed deals, and Blackstone's own real estate division is still recovering from the brutal stretch.

Slower fundraising and a deal lull have fueled a boom on secondaries for real estate and other private assets. Real estate secondaries deals led by fund managers rose to $14.5 billion last year, up 60% from a year earlier, according to a report from Ares Management Corp. The report projected volumes to double over the next several years.

Continuation vehicles, which move existing assets into a new fund and raise fresh cash, have become increasingly popular among fund managers. In real estate, private-asset firms have increasingly used these funds to hold onto trophy assets in sectors such as logistics, data centers and senior housing, according to a report from Jefferies Financial Group Inc.
