Fed’s Waller leans towards 25bp hike next month, says would consider 50 bp if inflation is hot for October

2026-11-12

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/feds-waller-leans-toward-quarter-point-rate-hike-in-december-open-to-50-basis-points-090000672.html

Federal Reserve governor Chris Waller said Tuesday that he is leaning toward raising interest rates by a quarter percentage point in December. If October inflation data released Tuesday morning is hot, he's willing to raise rates by 50 basis points next month.

"Inflation is too high … this means that even if, as I expect, the monthly rate of inflation improves in October, I am leaning toward supporting an increase in our policy rate of 25 basis points at the FOMC's December meeting," Waller said in a speech in London.

But if the October inflation data comes out higher than expected, then stronger action may be needed, Waller said. "In that case, I believe a 50-basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting," he said. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."

The Consumer Price Index is expected to rise 0.3% in October compared to a month earlier, a far slower pace than September's 0.6% gain. "Core" inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, is seen rising 0.2%, down from 0.3% in the prior month.

Inflation, as measured by the Personal Consumption Expenditures index, the Fed's preferred gauge, came in hotter than expected. Core PCE inflation, excluding food and energy prices, rose 0.3% in September and 3.1% from that time last year.

"Let's just say it: September's inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining," Waller said.

Waller noted concerns about the breadth of price increases and said he'll be watching to see whether elevated inflation across many categories persists, including whether prices for high-tech goods related to the AI build-out continue to rise at the very high rate seen in recent months. Waller is also looking to see whether the run-up in energy prices over the past several months is being passed through to the cost of goods and services directly or indirectly.

Waller noted that the impact of higher prices from AI doesn't appear to be fading anytime soon, based on research by Federal Reserve staff. He pointed out that the index for high-tech products rose in December 2025 and jumped further in January 2026, ahead of an escalation in prices for those goods that eventually showed up in the February producer prices and inflation data.

He also said that the latest manufacturing data pointed to the possibility of further price increases and that there are reasons to expect energy prices will remain high due to higher insurance rates for ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz. He also noted that diesel oil production is constrained by limited refining capacity and is expected to remain high even in a more peaceful Middle East, unlike crude oil prices.

Markets are pricing in a better than 80% chance that the Fed hikes by a quarter of a percentage point.
