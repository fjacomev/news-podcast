US credit card debt reaches a record $1.3 trillion as inflation rises

2026-11-12

Por Brooke DiPalma

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/us-credit-card-debt-reaches-a-record-13-trillion-as-inflation-rises-165616476.html

Americans are leaning on credit cards to keep up with higher prices, with October's headline inflation data coming in hotter than expected.

In a new report from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, US credit card balances rose by $22 billion in the third quarter, reaching a record $1.28 trillion dollars in total debt. This increase came as inflation measured by the Consumer Price Index rose by 0.4% in October from the previous month.

Prices for key essentials like groceries increased 0.4% month over month after remaining relatively flat over the past two months. Americans also paid more for gas, fuel, and electricity, up 1%, 2.4%, and 0.9%, respectively, compared to the month before and on top of modest gains in previous months.

The national average credit card interest rate currently stands at 19.71%, per Bankrate, the highest interest rate level since December 2025. That's likely causing Americans to accrue debt faster. Plus, Americans tend to take on more debt around the holidays, with US consumers planning to spend over $1 trillion during the key holiday months this year, according to the National Retail Federation.

Americans "continue to spend, despite high gas prices and the lowest sentiment we've ever seen, so it doesn't surprise us that that's going to carry through the holiday season," Ali Furman, PwC US consumer markets industry leader, told Yahoo Finance in September. "It's just a continuation of the trends."

Delinquency rates, or the rate of people with overdue payments, worsened in the third quarter too, increasing by 0.3 percentage points from the previous quarter to 6.87%.

It's not just credit card debt that Americans are finding themselves in; higher rates on mortgage, auto, and student loans are also taking a toll.

Mortgage balances increased by $257 billion last quarter, totaling $13.4 trillion at the end of September. The average price of a home in the US is now near $500,000 after spiking around this time last year, according to St. Louis Fed data.

Auto loan balances jumped by $21 billion in Q3 to $1.73 trillion as new vehicle prices hit a record high of $51,000 in October, according to Kelley Blue Book.

Student loan balances also grew by $5 billion, bringing the total to $1.66 trillion in debt for Americans as tuition continues to climb each month.

Higher interest rates may be here to stay, making it harder for Americans to pay off the debt. Based on the hotter-than-expected October inflation reading, the Fed is expected to raise interest rates at its policy meeting next month.
