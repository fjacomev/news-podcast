Winners and Losers From AI Agents

Key Takeaways

An estimated $30 trillion in consumer spending could be digitized through agentic AI offerings across multiple industries.

As AI agents increasingly influence purchasing decisions, operational efficiency, pricing and digital capabilities may become more important than traditional advantages such as brand recognition.

AI agents could make it easier for consumers to compare products and services, intensifying competition in industries such as insurance, auto retail and ticket resale.

By simplifying discovery, booking and transactions, AI agents could increase the use of software for such industries as transportation, local services and insurance.

Established retailers with trusted customer relationships, competitive prices and reliable delivery networks may be better positioned to withstand disruption.

The recent launch of powerful consumer AI agents has reignited debate over how artificial intelligence could disrupt industries ranging from retail and insurance to transportation and live entertainment.

Unlike traditional AI tools that primarily answer questions, these agents can browse websites, complete forms, compare prices, make purchases and even help negotiate bills.

Morgan Stanley Research estimates that roughly $30 trillion in consumer spending could be digitized through AI agents across online advertising, e-commerce, travel, food delivery, ridesharing, logistics, wearables and other sectors.

The extent of the disruption will depend on how quickly consumers adopt AI agents and how effectively companies make money from agent-driven activity and transactions.

“We continue to believe leading tech platforms with broad-based reach and unique data sets are in advantaged positions to create unique consumer agentic offerings that drive utility and garner scaled adoption,” says Brian Nowak, head of U.S. Internet Research at Morgan Stanley.

For investors, the implications vary widely by industry. While AI agents could increase demand for certain services and make transaction more efficient, they could also intensify price competition, weaken traditional advantages in winning customers and shift advertising revenue toward new platforms.
