Fed releases meeting schedule for 2027, will not hold policy meeting next October

2026-11-13

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/fed-releases-meeting-schedule-for-2027-will-not-hold-policy-meeting-next-october-184022755.html

The Federal Reserve will not hold a policy meeting next October as part of a revised tentative schedule of policy meetings in 2027 released Thursday.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh announced last month that the interest-rate-setting committee would continue to meet eight times next year, but would scale back the number of policy meetings to seven, with one left for discussing broad monetary policy strategy issues. At the strategy meeting, there will be neither a vote on interest rates nor a policy statement released.

"Getting policy right involves assessing near-term developments at timely, regular, and information-rich intervals," Warsh told reporters last month. "But it's equally important we reserve time and space, as an intellectually diverse group, for strategic deliberations — for thinking anew about big questions, looking out over longer time horizons."

This is the first step in eventually scaling back the number of policy meetings to six — an idea the Fed chairman floated this summer that the rest of the Fed supports because it will allow time to collect a fuller set of data to make more informed decisions about interest rates.

Warsh expects that in 2028, the Fed will have two meetings dedicated to discussions of strategy and six for setting policy.

The change comes as the Fed increasingly finds itself receiving key data after it sets interest rates. Last month, the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) index was released on Oct. 29, the day after the Fed met. The PCE index was released within days after the April and July meetings as well. The December meeting will take place the day before the November Consumer Price Index report is released.

While they can use pieces of other inflation datasets to estimate this measure, if the central bank met every eight weeks instead of the current six, it would have a fuller set of data available at each meeting.

"The current calendar has clear drawbacks," Deutsche Bank chief US economist Matt Luzzetti previously told Yahoo Finance. "The Fed could meaningfully improve the timeliness of the data available to them at FOMC meetings with relatively simple tweaks to the calendar."
