Goldman Sees S&P 500 Rising 14% on Cheap Hyperscaler Valuations

2026-11-14

Por Julien Ponthus

(Bloomberg) -- Goldman Sachs Group Inc. strategists are bullish on US stocks for 2027, predicting the S&P 500 will advance 14% over the coming year after a selloff in the biggest technology companies left their valuations historically cheap.

The team led by Peter Oppenheimer are overweight on the US and Japan for next year. They said in a note to clients that they see the S&P 500 hitting 8,700 points in the next 12 months, even as they expect earnings growth for the benchmark to slow sharply. The S&P is up 11% so far in 2026.

Goldman's call comes after the Nasdaq 100 dropped 9% from its early October high, dragged down by concerns over the artificial-intelligence buildout and the scandal at Nvidia Corp. that cost Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang his job this week. The declines have left so-called hyperscalers trading "well below" their historical valuations, the strategists said.

"We see Technology — and the Hyperscalers in particular — as relatively defensive," they wrote. "True, the biggest companies have turned cash flow negative, but overall balance sheet measures such as net debt/equity remain healthy."

With global earnings growth expected to ease in 2027, the team recommended investing in US and Japanese stocks and in industries that typically thrive amid lower bond yields, easing inflation and falling oil prices. They forecast a rapid deceleration in earnings-per-share expansion, with EPS growth on the S&P 500 sliding to 11% next year from 36% in 2026, and to 7% from 18% for the Stoxx Europe 600.

That, in turn, means markets are likely to assign a premium to the companies that continue expanding rapidly, according to Goldman.

"We expect investors to seek companies that can sustain growth as it becomes more scarce," the Goldman strategists wrote. "Attractive opportunities are emerging in long-duration growth companies, which should become more valuable as growth rates slow."

The bank is underweight European stocks, with Oppenheimer's team arguing valuations haven't come off as much as in other regions, while government debt "issues" and "a heavy political calendar" mean risks are higher. The Stoxx Europe 600 hit a fresh record this week, boosted by an upbeat earnings season and investors rotating out of the AI trade.
