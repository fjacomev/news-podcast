PPI report: Inflation shows signs of broadening in red-hot wholesale prices, keeping pressure on Fed to hike rates

US wholesale prices surged to a four-year high in October as broad, sticky inflation across manufacturing and supply chains reinforced expectations that the Federal Reserve will hike interest rates by 25 basis points next month.

The Producer Price Index for final demand, which measures the prices that factories, farmers, and producers charge for their finished goods and services, rose 6.5% last month, the largest increase since the aftermath of the pandemic when prices jumped 7.4% in 2022. Expectations were for the wholesale price index to rise 6.6%.

On a core basis, excluding food, energy, and trade services, PPI rose 4.9% in October, compared with 5% in September, indicating that wholesale cost pressures persisted beyond the energy sector into other segments of the economy.

Fed officials are noticing.

Boston Fed president Susan Collins told Bloomberg TV Friday morning that she hadn't had a chance to do a deep dive on the PPI data, but that after receiving CPI data along with the wholesale price data that "we're actually seeing some broadening evidence of broadening of price pressures" and "it's clear the direction and the numbers are less promising than, you know, I might have hoped."

"It's clear the direction that monetary policy needs to go, and I would think that soon some additional firming tightening of policy is appropriate," she said.

Collins also said of the path for rates that "it may be that a bit of a tighter policy path than I had penciled in a couple of months ago might be appropriate, but I think how high and how quickly is something we're going to have to decide meeting by meeting."

Fed governor Michelle Bowman said Tuesday that with inflation showing signs of broadening, rate hikes may be needed to slow the pace of price increases.

"Inflation pressures have persisted for longer than I had hoped and have shown recent signs of broadening," Bowman said in a speech in Washington.

Hawkish stance from the Fed

Meanwhile, in the PPI report, a surge in transportation and warehousing prices underscored that higher diesel prices were still showing up.

This marked the second month in a row of exceptionally high wholesale prices, following a more than 6% rise in September.

Over 60% of the total price increase for physical goods came from everyday manufactured products, rather than from volatile categories like food or energy, though food prices jumped 1.3%. Energy prices dipped slightly by 0.1%, but still remained elevated.
