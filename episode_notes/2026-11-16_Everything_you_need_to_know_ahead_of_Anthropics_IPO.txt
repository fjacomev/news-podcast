Subject to regulatory clearance, Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) will officially hold its initial public offering and set its pricing on Wednesday, Nov. 18, the company announced Thursday. It will begin trading under the ticker ANT on Thursday, Nov. 19.

The artificial intelligence startup publicly filed its S-1 paperwork for its initial public offering — the prospectus a company must file with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) before selling stock to the public — on Oct. 26, 2026.

Before Anthropic's stock can begin trading, the company's executives and its underwriters must complete an investor roadshow, set a price, and receive a final green light from regulators. The IPO roadshow started on November 10.

The countdown is on for one of the largest IPOs in history.

FAQS

When did the roadshow begin?

Anthropic said it began its roadshow on Tuesday, Nov. 10. The company is seeking a valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion in its closely watched IPO.

Companies that let the SEC review a draft of their IPO paperwork in private are required to wait at least 15 calendar days after making it public before starting their formal roadshow — the meetings where bankers pitch the stock to big investors and take orders. Since Anthropic released its filing to the public on Oct. 26, Nov. 10 was the earliest possible date the roadshow could begin.

When will Anthropic set its IPO price?

Anthropic will set its IPO price on Nov. 18, 2026.

The maker of Claude disclosed on Tuesday in an updated filing that it will sell 825 million shares at between $70 and $78 each. The final price is usually set after investors have indicated their demand, the night before the stock starts trading.

On top of the public offering, Anthropic is locking down an extra $26 billion on the side from tech's biggest heavyweights, including Nvidia (NVDA), Microsoft (MSFT), Google (GOOG, GOOGL), TSMC (TSM), Broadcom (AVGO), AMD (AMD), and Micron (MU). Those companies are buying non-voting Class N shares at the same price public investors will pay. Because this is a private deal, those tech giants won't face the standard six-month lockup window that typically prevents insiders from selling right after the debut.

What could change the schedule?

Many factors. Market volatility, particularly in comparable stocks already trading, and any outstanding regulatory issues could shift the timetable.

Should you buy Anthropic stock on day one?

History says patience pays off. Of more than 9,000 IPOs from 1975 to 2021, 60% were flat or lower three years after their first day's closing price, according to data from University of Florida professor Jay Ritter that Yahoo Finance analyzed before SpaceX's debut.

Anthropic could get the same early boost SpaceX (SPCX) did from index funds forced to buy the stock. But that buying may have come at a price for SpaceX, which fell 15% in its first week in the Nasdaq-100.

Who stands to make money from the IPO?

Early investors and institutions backing Anthropic may be in for a significant windfall. Anthropic's S-1 filing showed two of Anthropic's biggest backers — Amazon (AMZN) and Alphabet — are likely to be winners.

Anthropic's venture capital backers, including Spark Capital, Menlo Ventures, Coatue Management, and Lightspeed Venture Partners, as well as funds managed by Fidelity and Blackstone, also stand to gain.

Some shareholders have already converted part of their holdings into cash ahead of the IPO. In April, Anthropic facilitated a tender offer in which shareholders, including CEO Dario Amodei, president Daniela Amodei, and other executives, sold approximately $4.66 billion in stock.

According to The Wall Street Journal, Dario and Daniela Amodei, as well as Anthropic's other five founders, each plan to cash out about $250 million in the IPO, leaving them with stakes worth around $18 billion each.

What information did the S-1 filing reveal about Anthropic?

Anthropic's public prospectus revealed some key details about the AI company's finances. For instance, Anthropic disclosed that its revenue grew $1,206% to $33.2 billion through the first nine months of the year that ended Sept. 30. Operating losses also improved year over year to -$1.3 billion in the first nine months of 2026 from -$5.2 billion in the same period in 2025.

It also outlined commitments to spend a staggering $895.4 billion, which will primarily go toward compute and infrastructure. Of that total amount, $69.8 billion will be due in the next 12 months.

Anthropic reiterated a series of risk factors that threaten its business, including a narrow customer base and runaway artificial intelligence that could pose "existential risks to humanity."
