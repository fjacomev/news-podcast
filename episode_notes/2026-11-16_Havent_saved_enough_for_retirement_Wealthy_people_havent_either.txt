Haven't saved enough for retirement? Wealthy people haven't either.

2026-11-16

Por Kerry Hannon

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/havent-saved-enough-for-retirement-wealthy-people-havent-either-192922069.html

The rich aren't that different from you and me.

They also agonize about having enough money set aside for retirement — even if the amount they need to "retire comfortably" is loftier.

Wealthy Americans say they will need $2.6 million to feel financially set to retire, up 26% from last year, according to a new study from financial services firm HSBC.

That savings target is roughly double the amount that less-wealthy workers aim for.

"For affluent households, it's not simply about accumulating a larger nest egg, but ensuring their wealth can sustain their desired lifestyle over several decades," Jessie Zhu, an HSBC managing director, told Yahoo Finance.

Savings lag behind

The problem is, wealthy savers are only halfway toward reaching their goal on average. Baby boomers, those nearest to retirement, have hit 74% of their target, while millennials clock in at just 42%.

That trend holds for those lower on the economic ladder, other surveys have found.

About half of workers surveyed by Schroders have less than $500,000 in savings at retirement — including 24% who say it's unlikely they will have $250,000 set aside.

Northwestern Mutual found that nearly half of workers it surveyed don't expect to be financially prepared and believe there's a good chance they'll outlive their savings.

Is delaying retirement a solution?

To help fill the coffers, nearly two-thirds of affluent Americans plan to stretch out their earnings years by slowly trimming back their working hours over time, the HSBC data found.

But formal phased retirement programs that offer a reduced or flexible schedule and a lighter workload have been few and far between. Last year, just 7% of firms surveyed by the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) offered a formal program. A larger share—about 2 in 10— offered an informal arrangement in which workers create their own plan with their manager.

Like wealthier Americans, working longer is the fallback position for most of us. More than 4 in 10 workers expect to work past age 65 or do not plan to retire, according to Transamerica research.

But the harsh reality is that most people retire before they reach 65 due to a health issue, caregiving for an aging relative, or a job loss.

So how much should you be saving? It depends on many variables, including where you live, how expensive your lifestyle is, your health, and your financial obligations.

In general, you should aim to have 10 times your pre-retirement income saved by the time you reach age 67. If you have an annual salary of $100,000, that means $1 million saved by the time you retire.
