By Joey Roulette

Nov 15 (Reuters) - A Vulcan rocket owned by United Launch Alliance, the joint venture of Boeing and Lockheed Martin, failed on Sunday shortly after liftoff from Florida, destroying its payload of 40 satellites from Amazon's Leo internet network in the launch company's first mission failure.

The rocket failed roughly 10 minutes after liftoff from the Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida, and the cause was not immediately clear. It represented a major setback for the two-decade-old company's new flagship rocket as ULA sought to begin a regular cadence of launches for Amazon's burgeoning satellite internet constellation.

"Based on data received by our Launch Director, we confirm that the Vulcan vehicle launched from Space Launch Complex 41 suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit," ULA said in a statement.

"Every flight generates data, and every flight teaches us something. Since the anomaly just occurred, we don't have any specific data yet to understand what occurred today," ULA said.

The failure is expected to lead to a lengthy technical investigation that would keep the rocket off the market for months. This could deepen a US launch bottleneck brought on by other rocket mishaps and challenges across the industry as well as the looming retirement of SpaceX's Falcon 9, the world's most active rocket. SpaceX is no longer accepting bookings for the Falcon 9.

"There is risk with any launch, and that's especially true for inaugural missions like LV-01," Amazon said in a statement, referring to the name of Sunday's mission. "ULA has been a trusted partner for Amazon, and we believe they'll learn from this mission and deliver on their long-term plans for Vulcan."

The Sunday flight was Vulcan's fifth since its 2024 debut and first in nine months, following a launch hiatus drawn out by separate technical investigations into its solid rocket side boosters and its core BE-4 engines, which are supplied by Amazon founder Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin space company.

Blue Origin is working with ULA in the investigation into Sunday's failure, according to a person familiar with the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity.

The boosters were being scrutinized for issues encountered on Vulcan's last two flights, including a mission in February for the US Space Force. Both missions successfully delivered their payloads to orbit, but the Pentagon had not yet approved the rocket to resume flying national security payloads, pending its performance with the Amazon mission on Sunday.
