Trump is headed to China this week. Jensen Huang won’t be in tow but Elon Musk will be.

2026-11-16

Por Ben Werschkul

President Trump is set to depart early Monday for a trip to Asia that includes stops in the Philippines and China for meetings focused on trade and other economic issues, but with the ongoing scandal at Nvidia hanging over the journey.

Jensen Huang is not scheduled to be along for the trip, even as Trump continues to voice support for the former Nvidia (NVDA) CEO. But the White House has confirmed that Tesla (TSLA) and SpaceX (SPCX) CEO Elon Musk will join during the China leg at least.

"He's a big part of our national security," a senior US official told reporters, adding that Musk will "be of tremendous benefit just even as an advisor to the president."

Musk is not the only business leader expected to cross paths with the president at a China-hosted CEO summit, one of the major stops of the week.

That gathering is being organized by a group called the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade, which announced Sunday that over 100 top global companies — including Apple (AAPL), Google (GOOG), Walmart (WMT), ExxonMobil (XOM), and Boeing (BA) — are set to be represented.

Mastercard (MA) CEO Michael Miebach will be another attendee, the South China Morning Post reported on Sunday, and Chinese state media added that 12 leaders of APEC economies will also attend.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will be on hand throughout the week and has signaled that his focus will be on establishing a new dialogue with China on AI to improve communication.

In a Fox News appearance on Sunday, Bessent raised new questions about what he called a "culture of noncompliance" at Nvidia that led to semiconductor exports potentially ending up in Chinese hands in recent years. He said Nvidia's board of directors should go outside the company for a new executive as part of a "dramatic shakeup."

The summit of business leaders occurs just ahead of an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) gathering being held on Nov. 18-19 in Shenzhen, China

Trump's first stop is a gathering of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) in the Philippines that opened over the weekend. The president is set to land in Manila on Tuesday evening, local time.

Trump is expected to sign a trade deal with Thailand as part of those meetings alongside what the White House is previewing as other "commercial deals," one of which is expected to focus on aviation.

A meeting between Trump and Xi — and questions of Chinese spying

Tariffs will be on the agenda for the second half of the trip, when the leaders of the world's two largest economies meet, Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping's third face-to-face meeting of the year. But there is little expectation of major shifts in the relationship.
