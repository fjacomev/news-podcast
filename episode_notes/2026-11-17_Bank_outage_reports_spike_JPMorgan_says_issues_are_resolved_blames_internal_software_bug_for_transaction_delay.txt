Bank outage reports spike: JPMorgan says issues are resolved, blames internal software bug for transaction delays

Reports of problems with banking apps and payment services surged across major US banks Monday, though several institutions said their systems were operating as usual while some customers experienced transaction delays.

Customers' reports of issues spiked across a number of sites, including those for JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America (BAC), US Bank (USB), Wells Fargo (WFC), PNC (PNC), Citigroup (C), and Capital One (COF), according to outage aggregator Downdetector. Digital payment network Zelle, which is owned by Early Warning Services, also saw reports of customers experiencing issues.

Reports of problems began declining by Monday afternoon with complaints remaining elevated across several banks until the evening. Downdetector tracks user-submitted reports, which do not necessarily indicate outages at the financial institutions themselves.

JPMorgan Chase said a software bug affecting its internal hardware systems caused transaction delays Monday.

The nation's largest bank said the problem was not systemic, but acknowledged that other financial institutions may have experienced transaction delays when processing payments involving JPMorgan.

"This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us," a JPMorgan spokesperson told Yahoo Finance.

The bank attributed the disruption to a "software bug in on-premise hardware systems," adding that there was no evidence the issue was related to a cyberattack. Customers continued to have access to their accounts and funds, the spokesperson said.

JPMorgan said Monday evening that it has resolved the issue.

Several other major US banks also saw a surge in customer outage reports on Monday. Some of the companies with reported service outages, acknowledged customer issues while saying they're operations were running as normal.

"U.S. Bank systems are not directly impacted. However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays," a US Bank spokesperson said.

An Early Warning spokesperson said the payment network itself was in regular operation, despite some customers experiencing delays with payments made through the service.

"Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should," the spokesperson said. "An issue at the financial institutions may be impacting broader services, including Zelle payments, for some of their customers," they added.

Other banks including Bank of America, PNC, Capital One and Truist whose customers reported issues said they were not experiencing disruptions.
