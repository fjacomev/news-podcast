Blackstone REIT to Buy Amazon Data Centers in $5 Billion Deal

(Bloomberg) -- Blackstone Inc.'s newly listed data center acquisition vehicle has acquired a majority interest in two joint ventures with Amazon Web Services in a transaction valued at roughly $5 billion.

In its first-ever investment, Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust Inc. will own 13 newly constructed powered shells across two AWS data center campuses in northern Indiana and Mississippi, according to Nick Pell, chief executive of the fund known as BXDC.

BXDC will acquire a majority interest of 75% and AWS will retain 25%, and the hyperscaler will lease back the whole portfolio with an initial term of 12 years extendable to 32 years, according to a statement Monday.

"This direct deal with Amazon creates a roadmap to do more such deals with hyperscalers or developers," Pell said in an interview. The joint venture has a debt commitment to borrow 50% of the purchase price at close, according to Pell. The transaction is expected to be completed this year.

The real estate investment trust encapsulates Blackstone's ambition to become one of the largest backers of artificial intelligence infrastructure and its push to reach more individual investors. The nearly $1.4 trillion alternative asset manager has amassed a $200 billion data center portfolio that includes QTS Realty Trust, a leading builder and operator of such facilities.

Listed in May, BXDC has set out to acquire stabilized data centers valued between $250 million and $1.5 billion and with long-term lease contracts with hyperscalers, an addressable market the fund estimates at $1 trillion over the next five years.

"There are a lot of developers and close-ended funds looking to exit and recycle their capital and we are finding a home for them with the public investor capital in BXDC," Pell said.

The Democratic sweep in midterm elections is set to give momentum to the party's drive to set guard rails on the growth of data centers. Development of the facilities has come up against organized opposition from communities as well as supply shortages for equipment, creating bottlenecks.

More than 60% of the data center capacity planned for completion in 2027 has yet to begin construction, according to a May report from JPMorgan Chase & Co.
