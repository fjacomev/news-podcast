Google Parent to Take Part in $3 Billion in Prepaid Energy Deals

2026-11-17

Por Erin Hudson; Amanda Albright

(Bloomberg) -- Google parent Alphabet Inc. is planning to participate in two upcoming prepaid energy transactions totaling $3 billion.

Alphabet is listed as the funding recipient on two bond offerings that will be issued by the Southeast Energy Authority, according to preliminary bond documents posted Monday. Both transactions are being managed by Goldman Sachs Group Inc., one of the leading underwriters of prepaid deals.

The deals come about two weeks after Alphabet's last transaction. It was the funding recipient on a roughly $1.6 billion deal that priced Nov. 4.

Prepaid energy bonds are a fast-growing sector in the tax-exempt debt market for local and state governments. The sector has already set a new record for annual issuance with about $40 billion in prepaid energy bonds sold so far this year, compared to the prior record of $31 billion last year, according to data compiled by Bloomberg.

Spokespeople for Google and Goldman both declined to comment.

Prepaid deals are complex transactions that allow utilities to secure a discounted supply of electricity or gas. They involve a financial middleman, known as a funding recipient, which receives the proceeds of the bond sale upfront in exchange for making payments over time that facilitate the delivery of the energy to the participating utility.

The utility in both upcoming SEA transactions is the Salt River Project Agricultural Improvement and Power District, which provides electricity to customers in Arizona's Maricopa, Gila and Pinal counties.

Part of the prepaid sector's growth is attributed is the growing number of companies that are willing to participate in the transactions as funding recipients. Historically, the role was played by banks, but now firms including insurance companies, a real estate investment trust and most recently, Alphabet have stepped into the market as funding recipients.

Alphabet's first time serving as a funding recipient in a prepaid energy deal was in June. The deal was the first time a US tech company was known to play such a role and signaled a major evolution in the sector for investors and bankers.
