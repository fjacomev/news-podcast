Nvidia is being pulled in opposite directions by 2 powerful forces

2026-11-17

Por Hamza Shaban

https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/nvidia-is-being-pulled-in-opposite-directions-by-2-powerful-forces-110000361.html

Everyone's social feeds are a little different. So depending on your own views towards China, open-weight AI models, Silicon Valley founders, and the role of Big Tech in society, the idea of Jensen Huang returning to Nvidia (NVDA) is a brilliant, righteous comeback, a threat to national security, or the height of corporate irresponsibility.

Nvidia is reportedly waving off the comeback. Its efforts are directed instead to the investigation into Huang and navigating a regulatory landscape that's more hostile to Chinese competition. But regardless of the company's public posture, it is being pulled in opposite directions by very powerful forces.

Much of the tech world is clamoring for Huang's return. Meanwhile, members of Congress are sharpening their oversight knives. And in an extraordinary show of Cabinet infighting, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent accused Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick of failing to block China from getting ahold of Nvidia's most advanced chips.

Part of what has made Huang's reported affairs and resignation such a big story is that the saga contains so many competing storylines.

A sex scandal with the power to rattle the S&P 500 has the narrative juice to entice your politics-brained dad, your day-trading brother, and your Real Housewives-obsessed bestie.

But the varying interests drawing people to the story also highlight political and financial tensions that make Nvidia so important to the economy.

Huang is simultaneously occupying the role of founder hero, traitor to his country, China dove whipping boy, and free-market bulwark.

The competing narratives are proving too much for the stock. Tickers typically aren't supposed to become the stage for so much messiness. At least 10 analysts downgraded their Buy rating on Nvidia to Hold. Shares are down roughly 13% over the past week.

An earnings call after the bell will attract even more attention. Investors expect some clarity on the status of the search for a permanent CEO and Nvidia's role in China's acquisition of advanced AI tech. And, given the quarter's high expectations, like revenue nearly doubling from the same period last year, according to Bloomberg analyst consensus, the results will no doubt underscore the opposing forces pulling on the company.
