Nvidia’s board can replace Jensen Huang — replacing the handshakes behind $279 billion in supply deals is harder: Chart of the Day

2026-11-17

Por Jared Blikre

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-can-replace-jensen-huang-but-not-the-handshakes-behind-its-chips-chart-of-the-day-141658786.html

A Jensen Huang comeback is a 1-in-9 long shot on prediction markets, and Dan Ives calls it "ridiculous." Nvidia's (NVDA) supply chain, built on his handshakes, makes it less ridiculous than it sounds.

Days after Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO amid affair scandals, the Financial Times reported Sunday that he has discussed a return, and allies are building a Silicon Valley "war room" to lobby the board.

Nvidia stock is on track for its lowest close since early July 2025, about a third below its October record. The company's first earnings report without Huang comes Tuesday after the market close.

Yorkville's Ives said "no one individual can replace Jensen as CEO," but he called a comeback "ridiculous in our view given the legal/Board/DC scrutiny/moral compass/China tentacles."

The case for Huang starts with paperwork that doesn't exist. He has said Nvidia has never signed a contract with chipmaker TSMC (TSM) in three decades of business. Elon Musk, who backs a petition to bring Huang back, wrote on X that many deals "were a handshake with Jensen, not a written contract" and that "without Jensen, at least some of those critical deals will be lost."

The scale is staggering. One Vera Rubin rack, Nvidia's newest AI system, holds 1.3 million parts, per the company's count, from a supply chain twice the size of the one behind Blackwell. More than 150 partners in Taiwan alone help build it.

Nvidia had lined up $279 billion in supply and capacity commitments as of July, according to its latest quarterly filing. That's 17 times the total in January 2024. About $267 billion comes due in the next 2.5 years, and some of those deals can be canceled or adjusted before firm orders are placed.

Bettors took notice. Polymarket odds that Huang takes any formal role at Nvidia by year-end nearly doubled to about 20% after the FT report, then drifted down to about 11% Monday morning.

Nvidia's own annual report warns it is "highly dependent on the services of our longstanding executive team." And Huang ran the company with about 60 direct reports.

"How can you have the world's most valuable company and no kind of succession plan, not even anyone being groomed, [and] 60 people reporting to Jensen?" former Medtronic chair and CEO Bill George told Yahoo Finance.

The board isn't rolling out the welcome mat for Huang. It has approached Arm (ARM) CEO Rene Haas about the job, the FT reported, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants a dramatic shake-up with an outside CEO.

Not everyone buys the indispensable-founder pitch. "I'm not Jensen … but I know how little of the actual work that I do," Cisco chair and CEO Chuck Robbins told Yahoo Finance, pointing to Nvidia's "42,000 employees that wake up every day and go to the office and do a great job."
