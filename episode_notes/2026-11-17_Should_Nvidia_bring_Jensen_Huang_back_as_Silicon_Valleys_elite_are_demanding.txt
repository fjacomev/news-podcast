Something pretty remarkable is happening in Silicon Valley right now. One shouldn't be surprised, considering the profit motives of many invested in the AI ecosystem and that what Nvidia (NVDA) does lies at the center of it all.

Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned as the company's CEO under a cloud of controversy he created, and suddenly some of the biggest names in Silicon Valley are organizing a campaign to bring him back.

Nvidia stock slipped 0.3% before the bell on Tuesday.

Tesla (TSLA) and SpaceX (SPCX) CEO Elon Musk has signed on to the "Bring Jensen Back" petition. Other prominent executives have joined, including Uber (UBER) founder Travis Kalanick — who left Uber unceremoniously after his own string of highly publicized missteps.

Let's be clear: Jensen Huang is an extraordinary entrepreneur. A once-in-a-lifetime type of talent. He helped build Nvidia into one of the world's most important companies, with a market cap that neared $5 trillion earlier this year. His impact on the AI revolution is enormous and will not be forgotten.

But here's the issue a lot of people are right to point out: Being a brilliant CEO does not make you untouchable.

Huang admitted to being unfaithful to Lori, his wife of more than 40 years. There are serious questions surrounding his relationship with a reported Chinese spy. Additional reports say Jensen had affairs with two Nvidia employees, one of whom, Kimberly Powell, was a direct report.

All of these allegations and the broader circumstances deserve a thorough, independent investigation by the board and the law firm leading it, Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz. They should not be treated as proven facts.

And that's precisely the point.

This isn't just about one man's personal life. It's about corporate governance, national security, the responsibilities of a CEO, and the standards expected of someone running one of America's most strategically important companies.

Nvidia's board has a responsibility to shareholders, employees, and the country — not to Silicon Valley's celebrity CEO culture.

Jensen Huang absolutely deserves due process. But he is not bigger than Nvidia. And no CEO, no matter how brilliant, should be.
