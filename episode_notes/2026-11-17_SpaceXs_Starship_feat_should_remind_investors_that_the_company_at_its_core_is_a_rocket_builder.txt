SpaceX's Starship feat should remind investors that the company at its 'core' is a rocket builder

2026-11-17

Por Pras Subramanian

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/spacexs-starship-feat-should-remind-investors-that-the-company-at-its-core-is-a-rocket-builder-153734886.html

It was a massive day for SpaceX (SPCX) — and the rocket industry.

On Saturday, the company "caught" the upper stage, or Starship, of its largest rocket for the first time. The mission — Starship 15 — took off on Friday night, with Starship deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites and orbiting Earth several times before returning to Starbase, Texas, where it was caught by the Mechazilla platform.

It wasn't a perfect launch, as the lower-stage Super Heavy booster experienced an issue and had to abort its catch. CEO Elon Musk said the Super Heavy booster was slightly low on fuel, hence the abort, but that anomaly is essentially a "footnote in an otherwise historic mission," William Blair analyst Louie DiPalma wrote on Monday morning.

DiPalma added, "In our view, this Starship catch will take the throne for the most famous catch in history from Willie Mays 1954 World Series catch in Major League Baseball's fall classic."

Evercore ISI analyst Kutgun Maral wrote that the Starship 15 launch should remind investors that SpaceX is, at its "core," a rocket builder, and that should underpin much of its value.

"While the never-ending SpaceX newsflow and its stock implications keep us busy day to day, the long-term value of this company will be decided less by the next GW of compute or the next contract renewal than by the cost and frequency of access to orbit," Maral wrote in a note to investors. "That is why Starship has been at the center of our thesis since our initiation — it underpins aspirations for broadband, mobile, orbital compute and, ultimately, the mission itself."

But it was also a "tale of two cities" for the space sector this weekend, as Blair's DiPalma said, flagging United Launch Alliance's setback with its Vulcan rocket on Sunday, with an "anomaly" leading to a lost rocket.

The Vulcan rocket, ULA's newest, lost all 40 Amazon Leo (Project Kuiper) broadband internet satellites on board. ULA is a joint venture between Boeing (BA) and Lockheed Martin (LMT).

"We will work closely with our customer and other appropriate organizations to find the cause, determine the appropriate corrective action, and return to flight as quickly as possible," ULA said in a statement.

"ULA's struggles have taken place during an era in which SpaceX has perfected reusable launch. Over the past 3 years, the Vulcan system has completed 4 successful missions compared with nearly 500 for SpaceX," DiPalma wrote.

Prior to this weekend's mishap, ULA achieved more than 150 consecutive successful launches before experiencing its first total mission failure on Sunday.
