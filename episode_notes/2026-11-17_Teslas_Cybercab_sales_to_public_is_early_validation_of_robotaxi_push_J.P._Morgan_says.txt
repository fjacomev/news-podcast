Tesla’s Cybercab sales to public is ‘early validation’ of robotaxi push, J.P. Morgan says

2026-11-17

Por Pras Subramanian

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/teslas-cybercab-sales-to-the-public-are-early-validation-of-robotaxi-push-jpmorgan-says-192145629.html

A private chauffeur for less than the cost of an average car? This is what Tesla (TSLA) promised, and it's coming, with a couple of catches.

Tesla started selling its driverless Cybercab to private buyers, but only to a hand-picked few, and only if they agree to a monthly fee on top of the sticker price.

"Select customers are now being invited to purchase Cybercab for personal use," Tesla's Robotaxi account posted on X Friday.

The two-seater, which has no steering wheel or pedals, costs $29,990 before taxes. Buyers also put down a $1,000 nonrefundable deposit and sign up for a $299-per-month "Autonomy Subscription," with the first month free. That works out to nearly $3,600 a year before insurance or maintenance.

Deliveries are estimated for December, and the car can only operate within Tesla's Robotaxi service areas in Texas: Austin, Houston, Dallas, Fort Worth, and San Antonio.

The car is for personal use only, and Tesla hasn't said whether owners will be able to add it to its Robotaxi network to earn money, a longtime Musk promise.

JPMorgan analyst Rajat Gupta sees this as an early sign the self-driving bet is working. Tesla's rapid robotaxi ramp, "including this weekend's launch of customer Cybercab sales, is starting to present early validation of the company's unique advantage in physical AI," Gupta wrote in a note to clients Sunday.

Gupta expects customer-owned vehicles running unsupervised Full Self-Driving [FSD] to become "another, capital-light source of supply" for the network starting in the second quarter of 2027. Scale is also important, as JPMorgan ties 40% of its sum-of-the-parts Tesla valuation to robotaxis, calling progress there "a key fundamental support." Tesla now has 1,306 registered robotaxis in Texas, compared with 1,321 for Alphabet's Waymo.

Building scale and increasing production of the Cybercab is the reason why Elon Musk said Tesla was shutting down production of the Cybertruck in mid 2027, though some argue it had more to do with poor sales of Tesla's controversial pickup.

Be that as it may, the Cybercab payoff will take time. Gupta expects the earnings contribution "to remain limited during the initial build," with revenue inflecting in 2028. JPMorgan rates Tesla Neutral with a $430 price target.
