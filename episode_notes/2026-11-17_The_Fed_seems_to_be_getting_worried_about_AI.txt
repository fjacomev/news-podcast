The Fed seems to be getting worried about AI

2026-11-17

Por Hamza Shaban

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/the-fed-seems-to-be-getting-worried-about-ai-110000457.html

Thinking through the potential consequences of the AI revolution isn't explicitly part of the Fed's dual mandate.

But the sweeping impacts of AI, and all the corners of the economy that the emerging technology is set to disrupt, have drawn increasing scrutiny from central bankers.

On Monday, Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh kicked off the first task force hearing on how AI could juice GDP growth or lead to higher unemployment.

Warsh notably did not take the posture of industry booster or tech critic. "Our role in all this is neither advocacy nor opposition," Warsh said. "Our job is to prepare for whatever comes next."

That means focusing on AI's possible impacts and considering different outcomes, ranging from broad-based economic gains to massive job displacement to an AI letdown and productivity slump.

Warsh noted that AI might be different from other paradigm-shifting technologies because of its propensity to generate new ideas and speed up the pace of innovation.

In the best scenario, Warsh told the Fed conference in San Francisco, the technology could lead "to an increase in productivity and economic gains that are shared broadly across labor and the owners of capital. In that event, real GDP growth rises persistently above its long-run average, and the US stays close to full employment."

But given the Fed's specific objectives — inflation and employment — the risks overshadowed the optimism.

Warsh said that the potential for higher unemployment and the dangers of bad actors using AI for nefarious purposes could be "highly disruptive and economically costly."

The chair also referred to AI using the language preferred by Donald Trump and Elon Musk, calling it "super intelligence."

Other central bankers went further. Federal Reserve governor Michael Barr on Monday laid out an array of risks posed by AI. He elaborated on the dangers posed to the financial system if AI technology continues to advance and is widely adopted, such as AI-fueled bank runs, AI trading models that encourage herd-like behavior, and security concerns around autonomous agents exploiting vulnerabilities.
