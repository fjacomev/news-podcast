The S&P 500’s shot at 4 straight double-digit years is in Nvidia’s hands

2026-11-17

Por Jared Blikre

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/the-sp-500s-shot-at-4-straight-double-digit-years-is-in-nvidias-hands-191852498.html

Stocks are within reach of their fourth straight year of double-digit gains, the first since the 1990s dot-com boom. Apple (AAPL) is doing the heavy lifting, while Nvidia (NVDA) could still cost the S&P 500 (^GSPC) its four-peat.

The S&P 500 closed Monday up about 8% for 2026, roughly 110 points shy of the 7,530 or so it needs to end the year with a 10% gain. It had just closed above that line as recently as Friday.

BofA's Michael Hartnett counts only three such streaks since 1871, according to a BofA Global Research report. Those came during World War II, right after it, and in the late-1990s internet boom, when the index "logged 5 straight years" of 10%-plus gains.

Apple carries, Nvidia drags

Apple has added about 125 points to the S&P 500 this year, more than any other stock, by Yahoo Finance calculations. Chipmakers Micron (MU) and AMD (AMD) come next, at about 50 points each.

Nvidia sits at the other end. Its slide has subtracted about 75 points, roughly two-thirds of the gap between Monday's close and the 10% line. Tesla (TSLA), Oracle (ORCL), and Alphabet (GOOG, GOOGL) have each taken away about 25 to 40 points.

Nvidia gets the next say

Nvidia reports its third quarter results after the close on Nov. 17, its first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO.

At about 6% of the index, according to Carson Group's Sonu Varghese, every 10% move in Nvidia is worth roughly 40 points. Even a big post-earnings jump couldn't close the roughly 110-point gap alone.

The rest of the market has already done its part. More than half of S&P 500 stocks are up at least 10% this year.

Since the S&P 500's Oct. 6 record, chip stocks have shed about $5.5 trillion in market value, more than the roughly $3.7 trillion the whole index has lost. Gains in Apple and Eli Lilly (LLY) have softened the blow.

"I don't think the stock market overall is expensive," Pershing Square CEO Bill Ackman said at Yahoo Finance Invest. "I think there are a lot of stocks that have been obliterated and are at attractive valuations."

DoubleLine's Jeffrey Gundlach isn't buying it. He recommends "only 30% in equities" until the S&P 500's price-to-earnings ratio falls below 15.

History still gives the bulls a shot

Since 1928, the S&P 500 had been up 10% or more by mid-November in 45 years, and every one of them finished with a double-digit gain, by Yahoo Finance calculations. This year was in that club at Friday's close.

From here, the index needs to gain about 1.5% by Dec. 31. Since 1990, it has done that over the final six weeks in 20 of 36 years.
