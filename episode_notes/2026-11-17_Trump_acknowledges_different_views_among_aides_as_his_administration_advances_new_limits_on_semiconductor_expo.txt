Trump acknowledges 'different views' among aides as his administration advances new limits on semiconductor exports

The Trump administration announced on Monday that it is moving to close a perceived loophole in US export controls for advanced semiconductors as bipartisan questions about the role of Nvidia (NVDA) in possible national security lapses intensify.

The raging debate has split the Trump administration over how the US government should respond to the scandal at the chipmaker, with President Trump acknowledging that there are "two different views" among his aides.

Trump said some are leery of insulting China with harsh new restrictions, while others want to crack down. The president said he will make the final decision about how his government approaches the issue.

"I don't think you want to insult China just before you get there," Trump added before he departed for a trip to Asia that includes a China stop. "We're having a very good relationship with China right now." He did not address the new export rules directly.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, which oversees export control measures, advanced a rule to limit China's ability to access advanced chips that reside in data centers outside its borders.

The "interim final rule," the release noted, was "written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council" and moves the restrictions one step closer to being enacted.

The rules would limit access to sensitive American technology by cracking down on remote computing arrangements that allow Chinese companies to rent computing power in third countries.

It is a move to close what many see as a loophole that has allowed China to sidestep bans and access advanced chips provided by companies like Nvidia.

The announcement from Secretary Howard Lutnick's Commerce Department came as Lutnick faces bipartisan questions on whether he has slow-walked efforts in recent years to limit chip exports and even drawn criticism from other members of the Trump Cabinet.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has notably declined to defend Lutnick's actions in a series of recent television appearances. On Fox News on Sunday, Bessent also discussed what he termed a "culture of noncompliance" at Nvidia, which he said led to semiconductor exports potentially ending up in Chinese hands.

Bessent said Nvidia needs "a dramatic shake-up" and should "go outside the company"  as it seeks a new chief executive.
