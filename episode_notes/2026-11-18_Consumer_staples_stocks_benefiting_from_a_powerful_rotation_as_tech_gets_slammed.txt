Consumer staples stocks benefiting from a powerful rotation as tech gets slammed

2026-11-18

Por Brian Sozzi

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/consumer-staples-stocks-benefiting-from-a-powerful-rotation-as-tech-gets-slammed-173929452.html

Investors continue to find comfort in boring consumer staples stocks as growth expectations for the tech giants driving the AI boom come under closer scrutiny.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC) tech sector's (XLK) forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio is currently 17.2 times, its lowest level since 2020, according to data crunched by Duality Research. The S&P 500's consumer staples (XLP) sector forward P/E ratio of 24.2 times is at the highest level since 1999.

The S&P 500 consumer staples and tech sectors are up 18.7% and 12.7%, respectively, on the year.

"Is staples the actual bubble here? The sector's years to date gains are almost entirely attributable to multiple expansion," Duality Research noted.

Investors are rotating aggressively into consumer staples stocks to seek shelter from the sharp sell-off in high-flying technology and artificial intelligence shares.

Persistent inflation and elevated borrowing costs are squeezing household budgets, pushing shoppers to prioritize everyday necessities over discretionary upgrades.

Companies like Costco (COST), Procter & Gamble (PG), and Coca-Cola (KO) sell essential goods, and their cash flows and pricing power remain resilient during economic slowdowns. Additionally, these mature businesses offer reliable dividend yields that attract capital during periods of broader market volatility.

As institutional funds rebalance away from crowded momentum trades, staples have emerged as a safe-haven trade.

You can see it in the tape: P&G shares are up 9% over the past month, Coca-Cola is trading near a record high, and even struggling Campbell's (CPB) has seen its stock climb 20% in the past month.

Despite higher prices for consumer goods, consumers haven't retrenched — which has also helped sentiment on staples.

"The US consumer is remarkably resilient," P&G CFO Andre Schulten told Yahoo Finance in late October.

Schulten said the business is being powered by higher-income households, while the lower end is being more cautious.
