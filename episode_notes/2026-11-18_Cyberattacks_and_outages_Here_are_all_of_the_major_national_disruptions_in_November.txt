Cyberattacks and outages: Here are all of the major national disruptions in November

2026-11-18

Por Hamza Shaban

https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/cyberattacks-and-outages-here-are-all-of-the-major-national-disruptions-in-november-181143019.html

A new era of disruptive cyber outages is making the nonstop data breaches of just a few years ago seem quaint.

The recent cascade of breaks and digital dislocations feels like a preview to a more chaotic and disordered world, where everything is connected but also vulnerable and exploitable. AI is accelerating innovation, but it's also supercharging the capabilities of bad actors and increasing the blast radius for outages and overall online messiness.

Here's a brief rundown of what we've seen over the past week.

MicrosoftOutlook outage — Nov. 12

For about 36 hours, much of corporate America's productivity suite went offline. Microsoft's signature platforms, including Outlook and Teams, stopped working. More than 70 countries and territories experienced the outage, hampering millions of users.

"This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents," Microsoft explained. "The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change."

National flight grounding and FAA ATC outage — Nov. 13

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop for more than four hours, unleashing a wave of delays and cancellations across the country. Outdated air traffic communications equipment and copper cable upgrades were to blame.

While the stoppage lasted a handful of hours, its impacts rippled across air travel for several days as airlines scrambled to rebook passengers and get aircraft and crew back in place. The incident has become a political headache for the Trump administration and highlighted the nation's aging telecommunications infrastructure and its vulnerabilities.

JPMorgan (and others) banking outage — Nov. 16

Thousands of customers reported problems with their online banking services at several major institutions, tied back to a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase.

JPMorgan Chase said in a statement that it "has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems," adding that "there is no evidence this is cyber-related."

The disruption, coming on the heels of the other major outages, sparked concerns that the events were connected, and that hackers were to blame.

Users who made transactions involving Chase and other banks made it appear that the outage was systemic, but Chase said its banking platform was responsible.

In other cases, security researchers identified malicious code in online tools used by nearly 200 organizations, including reportedly Zoom, HSBC, and Equifax.

The attack targeted Optimizely, an AI platform that lets companies design websites. Researchers at the cybersecurity firm Report URI identified the attack on Monday.

In a post on its website Tuesday, Optimizely said that "an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability" in its Experimentation tool. "We've fixed the vulnerability and removed the injected code from affected projects," it said.

US National Security Comms network — Nov. 17

As we were writing about this, CNN reported that the US National Security Communications network has gone down.

Given that the NSA uses this network to communicate, it sounds like a big deal. On the other hand, President Trump says it's not,.

Neither CNN nor the NSA elaborated on the causes, but said there was currently no evidence of a cyberattack.

A spokesperson for the NSA gave the following statement: "The National Security Agency experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance. While some internal systems were affected, mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues. NSA's infrastructure is designed with multiple layers of redundancy to maintain operations during planned maintenance or unexpected events."

Schwab outage — Nov. 17

Charles Schwab users reported an outage on the brokerage app on Tuesday. More than 1,000 users reported a disruption, according to the website Downdetector.

Schwab said in a statement, "A brief webpage loading issue has been resolved. Trading remained available, and clients could access their accounts at schwab.com/login or through the Schwab Mobile app. We apologize for the inconvenience."

Apple iCloud outage - Nov. 17

According to Downdetector, a large suite of Apple (AAPL) services including iCloud, Apple Card, Sign in with Apple and others were experiencing outages. Apple confirmed the outage but didn't add any details.

3 risks not to ignore that suggest more of these are coming

As our rundown shows, there isn't one chief cause here despite the apparent acceleration in things breaking. But the web of causes is interconnected — just like our systems — and there is good reason to expect that things are getting worse with more cascades of breaks and digital dislocations.

Everything is connected, and everything is more vulnerable and exploitable.

Cybersecurity and hacking

The first and most obvious thing is cybersecurity. In the wake of the Binance hack — which was a major heist — founder Changpeng "CZ" Zhao told us that "banks are probably getting hacked left and right" as the cybersecurity landscape changes with AI-powered bad actors.

The big question in the wake of that hack was how much AI was involved. With powerful AI tools, more "zero-day" bugs are likely to be found exploited, more social engineering can happen at scale, and even more tracks can be covered — leaving vulnerabilities open for longer.

Aging and stressed systems

The bipartisan but continuously deferred dream of investing in American infrastructure doesn't just affect roads, but telecommunications networks and other critical systems. Deferred maintenance is bad enough, and the addition of inflated labor and material costs means that some stuff just doesn't get fixed until it's too late.

This was the gist of the FAA situation, as the failure of an aging copper switch highlighted a major failure of a system that's underfunded and not modernized. Those systems don't have to be mechanical either. It's no secret that the entire air traffic control system is understaffed, high-stress, and a potential recipe for problems.

Vibecoding and AI code productivity

The rise of tools like Claude Code is another potential pitfall for these outages. Never before has it been easier to "vibecode," and companies looking to swap out engineers with Claude subscriptions may be deploying code that's not quite ready for primetime.

This is, of course, speculation, as we haven't heard enough detail about any of these specific outages to point to this. What's not speculation, though, is that the sheer quantity of code output that's possible has meant the bottleneck is often now reviewing code — not writing it.

And with extremely heavy output expectations on engineers, the code equivalent of a typo going through to publication is a new, modern risk.
