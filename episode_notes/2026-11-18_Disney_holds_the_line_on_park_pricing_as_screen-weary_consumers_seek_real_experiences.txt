Disney holds the line on park pricing as screen-weary consumers seek 'real experiences'

2026-11-18

Por Brooke DiPalma

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/disney-holds-the-line-on-park-pricing-as-screen-weary-consumers-seek-real-experiences-165741949.html

Disney doesn't plan to change its park pricing anytime soon, as its experiences segment, including both parks and cruises, hit a record revenue last quarter.

"We feel very good about the current trends," CEO Josh D'Amaro said, adding that bookings for the first quarter in 2027 are higher compared to 2026.

"It's more than about just ticket price," D'Amaro said. "Consumers want real experiences now … we're seeing that accelerate. People today are spending so much of their lives on screens. They're just looking for time to connect with each other in the real world … Disney parks [are] one of the few places that you can actually do that."

Disney saw a record number of guests at its parks last quarter, the company said. D'Amaro added that Disney has more projects underway around the world than "any other point in history."

A lot of Disney's focus is on its Orlando resort, especially on Monstropolis at Disney's Hollywood Studios, Lake Shore Lodge, and Tropical Americas in Animal Kingdom. Disney also has plans to double the size of the Avengers Campus at Disneyland California by 2028.

NerdWallet found that a seven-night trip to Disney with a family of four could easily top $6,000 — and that's for a "frugal traveler."

In the most recent quarter, Disney reported an 11% increase in global guests visiting its parks and experiences, led by Disneyland Paris. Disney also increased capacity for its Disney Cruise Line following the launches of the Disney Adventure and Disney Destiny cruise ships.

Customers spent more at Disney parks too. Average spending per customer on admissions, food, and merchandise in US parks rose 4%, more than the expected 3.6% in the US. Internationally, sales at Disneyland Paris offset weakness in Hong Kong and Shanghai parks.

While the company is largely holding firm on prices, it is offering promotions and programs for Florida residents, its annual passholders, and Disney+ subscribers.

Disney is also exploring offering a free ad-supported version of its Disney+ streaming service.

"We are exploring a free product, and I think it will be more price-sensitive consumer and bring in new Disney+ subscribers," D'Amaro told investors. "It's going to give us more ad inventory to … offer a key retention tool that, quite frankly, we currently don't have."

A bundle deal for Disney+ and Hulu with ads currently costs $11.99 a month. Without ads, it costs $19.99 a month, and you're able to download movies and series. That could change soon.
