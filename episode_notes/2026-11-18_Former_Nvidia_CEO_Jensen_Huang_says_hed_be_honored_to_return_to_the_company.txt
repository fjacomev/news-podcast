Former Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang says he would be "honored" to return as chief executive of the company he co-founded.

In a post on X Tuesday, Huang apologized to his family again and commented on the support he's seen among former employees, customers, and others seeking to have the company's board reinstate him.

A person familiar with the matter told Yahoo Finance that Nvidia's board is not looking to bring Huang back.

"Since my departure, I've seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO," he wrote.

"Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

Huang resigned late last week following revelations of multiple affairs, including one allegedly involving a suspected Chinese spy. Two other affairs were allegedly with Nvidia employees. This is his first public statement since the scandal erupted.

Nvidia has named CFO Colette Kress the company's interim CEO.

Huang's resignation has raised questions about whether Nvidia will continue its prodigious investments across the AI industry, how it will define its roadmap in the years ahead, and more.

The company has shed hundreds of billions of dollars in its market valuation since the scandals erupted.

Huang is now scheduled to appear before closed-door sessions of Congress on Dec. 1 to discuss his reported relationship with the alleged spy, Alice Huang.

The company is scheduled to report its Q3 earnings after the bell today.
