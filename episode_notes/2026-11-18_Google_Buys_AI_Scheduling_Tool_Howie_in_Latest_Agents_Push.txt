(Bloomberg) -- Alphabet Inc.'s Google has bought Howie, an artificial intelligence-powered scheduling and calendar management tool, the tech giant's latest move to win over consumers with AI agents.

Google's experimental arm, Google Labs, announced the purchase Monday in an X post, without disclosing terms. Howie, whose tool lets users loop the AI agent into emails as they would a human secretary, was valued at $14 million in 2024, according to PitchBook data.

"We can't wait to learn from them as we build the next generation of intuitive AI tools together for small businesses," Google Labs said in the post.

Tech and AI companies have been intensely focused on developing AI agents that can do things on behalf of the user, an advancement beyond question-and-answer chatbots. In May, Google released Gemini Spark, a 24/7 agent that proactively completes digital tasks such as scan an inbox for emails, organize files or build a spreadsheet. That market is growing increasingly crowded, with offerings from OpenAI, Anthropic PBC, Meta Platforms Inc. and Elon Musk's xAI.

Petersmith and Google Labs did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the deal terms, or whether Howie will eventually be integrated into Google apps and services.

Google Labs has previously acquired AI tools built on Google technology. In February, it bought ProducerAI, a music generation platform that uses Google DeepMind's Lyria model.
