JPMorgan CFO Jeremy Barnum to step down as Jamie Dimon's succession plans take shape

2026-11-18

Por David Hollerith

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/jpmorgan-cfo-jeremy-barnum-to-step-down-as-jamie-dimons-succession-plans-take-shape-153110270.html

JPMorgan Chase (JPM) said Tuesday that longtime CFO Jeremy Barnum will step down next year in the latest change among the top ranks of the country's largest bank as it prepares for CEO Jamie Dimon's eventual succession.

Barnum, who has served as the bank's CFO since 2021, will be succeeded by Bregje de Best, CFO of JPMorgan's commercial and investment bank, after the company's first quarter earnings in April, according to a JPMorgan filing.

Barnum will become vice chair, remaining with the bank until early 2028, JPMorgan said. Dimon added in a press release statement that Barnum had requested the transition after six years in the role.

That tenure is slightly above average for a CFO at a large US company, according to a 2025 study by executive search firm Spencer Stuart, which found CFOs at S&P 500 companies had served an average of 4.8 years in their positions.

The announcement comes less than a week after Allison Beer, JPMorgan's head of credit card and connected commerce, informed colleagues that she was leaving to pursue another opportunity, according to an internal JPMorgan memo.

It also follows a broader leadership shake-up of Dimon's top lieutenants earlier this year when JPMorgan announced the retirement of Marianne Lake, the former head of its sprawling consumer bank and previously a leading candidate to succeed Dimon.

The announcement of Lake's departure also came as JPMorgan said it was elevating executives Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh to the role of co-president, signaling their leads to eventually take the reins from Dimon, who was expected to stay for three more years as CEO, according to an executive familiar with the matter who spoke to Yahoo Finance at the time.

Barnum's planned transition and Lake's retirement are part of a broader effort to prepare JPMorgan's next generation of leadership, according to a person familiar with the bank's succession planning.

The person characterized Beer's departure as separate, saying the credit card executive was seeking a broader leadership role than her current responsibilities at the bank.

De Best, 43, joined JPMorgan in 2006 and has held senior positions across investment banking, markets and corporate banking. She will also join the firm's operating committee when she assumes the CFO role.

Morgan Stanley analyst Manan Gosalia said Tuesday his team "do[es] not expect the CFO transition itself to have any impact on JPM's strategy or preliminary 2027 outlook."

RBC Capital Markets analyst Gerard Cassidy also told clients Tuesday, in reaction to Barnum's departure, that senior management shake-ups are not unusual at JPMorgan.

"We expect JPM's deep management bench will continue to enable the company to effectively manage through senior executive realignment," Cassidy said.
