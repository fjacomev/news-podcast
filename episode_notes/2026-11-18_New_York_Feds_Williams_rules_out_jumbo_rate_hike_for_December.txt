New York Fed’s Williams rules out jumbo rate hike for December

2026-11-18

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/new-york-feds-williams-rules-out-jumbo-rate-hike-for-december-205144565.html

New York Federal Reserve president John Williams poured cold water on the central bank making a jumbo 50-basis-point interest rate hike next month but noted that the Fed does have to get inflation down.

"I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action," Williams said during a Q&A with Axios in New York.

Williams acknowledged that the data is sending a "pretty clear signal."

"We still have our work to do to make sure that we bring inflation back down to 2% in a timely way and in a sustained manner," he said.

Williams' comments come as the latest inflation data shows price growth that is surprisingly higher than expected. The Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October, compared with expectations of 0.3%, marking a slight cooldown from September's 0.6% gain. "Core" inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, rose 0.3%, compared with expectations of 0.2% and unchanged from September.

A reading of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge showed prices heated up in September, with the core Personal Consumption Expenditures index rising 3.1%, up a tenth of a percentage point from 3% in August.

Markets have been weighing a larger rate hike ever since inflation readings came in hot. Fed governor Chris Waller said ahead of the CPI report that if consumer prices were higher than expected, he'd consider a move of 50 basis points.

After Williams spoke on Tuesday, investors still saw a 98% chance the Fed hikes by 25 basis points in December.

Williams wouldn't characterize inflation as broadening, but acknowledged a continuation of higher price increases on imported goods where tariffs are in place and energy prices that are being passed on. He also pointed to AI investments causing certain prices, such as semiconductors, to rise. Williams identified AI as the most likely "persistent" factor of inflation.

How many rate hikes and at what pace the Fed must implement them will depend on what's happening in AI, Williams said. He noted that a lot of strength in the economy is coming from AI, and consumer spending is being boosted by a strong stock market.

"If those continue, I think that that's a factor that will argue that we may need to raise rates more at the same time those factors have been pushing inflation up. But there's just a great deal of uncertainty," he said.
