There's 'no difference' between buying Anthropic's IPO and Amazon's IPO: Kevin O'Leary

2026-11-18

Por Paige Oamek

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/theres-no-difference-between-buying-anthropics-ipo-and-amazons-ipo-kevin-oleary-190707549.html

As Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) prepares to begin trading under the ticker ANT on Thursday, Nov. 19, business leaders are weighing in on the historically large initial public offering (IPO).

"There's no difference in investing in Anthropic on the IPO than Amazon on its IPO," "Shark Tank" host and O'Leary Ventures chair Kevin O'Leary said on Sozzi Unleashed. "There was tremendous volatility. It made zero money. But look at the platform it ultimately built. That's what you're betting on in the Anthropic deal."

Sitting down with Yahoo Finance's Brian Sozzi, O'Leary said the past few years haven't been great for buying IPOs, but he believes Anthropic may be a unique way to bet on artificial intelligence.

Anthropic said it expects to sell 825 million shares priced between $70 and $78 each. That would give the company a market valuation of between $1.3 trillion and $1.45 trillion — putting it near SpaceX's (SPCX) IPO valuation of $1.77 trillion, which was the largest ever.

"It may be the most hyped IPO ever," O'Leary said. "This IPO is the anchor to the entire AI or SI trade, whatever you want to call it."

What about the fact that Anthropic may not turn a profit until 2030? Though Anthropic's operating losses have improved, they still total $1.3 billion for the first nine months of the year. The company also noted in its amended IPO filing that it plans to spend nearly $900 billion on computing and data centers, with $70 billion of that total coming due within a year.

O'Leary said Anthropic's profitability is the wrong lens right now. Buying in is a bet on AI's role across all 11 sectors of the economy, and he sees evidence of productivity and margin gains so far. That has helped markets remain near all-time highs despite tariffs, wars, and high energy prices.

These remarks follow O'Leary's comments last month, when the businessman called Anthropic "a hot trade."

"I expect it to be liquid from day one, and that changes the opportunity," he wrote in October.
