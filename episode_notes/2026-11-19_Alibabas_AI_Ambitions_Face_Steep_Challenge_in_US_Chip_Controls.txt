(Bloomberg) -- Alibaba Group Holding Ltd. (BABA) is facing mounting pressure to show it can deliver on its artificial intelligence business plan despite new limits on its access to data center chips.

Its Hong Kong-listed shares capped a nine-day losing streak ahead of results due later Thursday from the internet firm. Alibaba's market value stands at $216 billion, less than half its level just over a year ago when investors were betting on it to lead the China's AI industry.

Draft US rules announced Monday would close a key workaround for Chinese hyperscalers to use advanced chips. That's making analysts turn more cautious on Chinese AI companies' cloud growth potential as well as their ability to train and run next-generation models.

"For Alibaba, they depend heavily on access to computing power, so this will be an important setback," said Matt Maley, chief market strategist at Miller Tabak. "It's likely going to create a situation where it will take longer for them to build an ecosystem that can compete with the most advanced American technology."

The new US rules are aimed at preventing Chinese firms from renting cutting-edge chips at data centres abroad. Alibaba and its peers are already banned from direct buying the most advanced processors, and Nvidia Corp. (NVDA) said this week it is no longer shipping lower-end Hopper data center chips to China as well.

Part of Alibaba's ambition to build 20 gigawatts of data center capacity before 2032 depends on expansion across regions like Southeast Asia. Chips made in house by its T-Head unit may provide some buffer, but still trail the performance of Nvidia's top products.

Fresh challenges aside, Alibaba's results are expected to show a continued drag on its e-commerce business from sluggish Chinese consumption and tough competition. Focus will be on whether that can be offset by its fast-growing cloud operation, which accounted for 18% of total revenue in the June quarter.

Among its peers, Tencent Holdings Ltd. posted results after Wednesday's close showing a negative profit impact from heavy AI spending. The company also warned that Washington curbs on Chinese access to overseas data centers will hamper development of its next AI model.
