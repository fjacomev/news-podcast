Fed minutes: Officials favored holding rates steady in October, but they see hikes ahead

2026-11-19

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/fed-minutes-officials-favored-holding-rates-steady-in-october-but-they-see-hikes-ahead-195719301.html

Almost all members of the Federal Reserve favored raising interest rates in the future, even as they decided to hold rates steady in October, according to minutes from the central bank's last policy meeting released Wednesday.

"With regard to the outlook for monetary policy in the months ahead, almost all judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate — broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections — would likely be appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2% objective," the minutes, which are released with a three-week lag, revealed.

The September Summary of Economic Projections, which includes 18 officials' expectations for the path of interest rates — excluding Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who doesn't pencil down his — showed one more rate hike this year, then holding rates steady throughout 2027.

Among those who favored holding rates steady at the last meeting, many said they wanted to gather additional information to help determine the best level of interest rates to bring inflation back down to their 2% goal in a timely way. These officials wanted to assess the durability of AI-related investment demand, the evolution of financing conditions, the transmission of the September rate increase to the economy, and geopolitical developments.

Some said that market expectations for rate hikes in December and next year were broadly consistent with their views on the path of interest rates, and several saw this alignment as reducing the risks of keeping the target range unchanged in October. A few felt holding rates steady was a close call.

Some participants favored raising rates at the last meeting, citing inflation remaining too high with few signs of it abating. They thought rates weren't high enough to restrict the economy and cool inflation, and a few warned that further delay risked allowing high inflation to become entrenched or necessitating a larger and more disruptive tightening later. Cleveland Fed president Beth Hammack, a voting member who publicly dissented last meeting, has made this argument. Fed governor Lisa Cook also dissented last meeting.

Several officials observed that the impact of tariffs on prices appeared to be waning, but most remarked that inflation pressures in the economy were broad-based. The vast majority noted that high energy prices continued to contribute to inflation pressures.
