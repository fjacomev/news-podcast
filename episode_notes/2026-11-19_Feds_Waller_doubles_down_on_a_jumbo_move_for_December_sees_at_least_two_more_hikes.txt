Fed’s Waller doubles down on a jumbo move for December, sees at least two more hikes

2026-11-19

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/feds-waller-doubles-down-on-a-jumbo-rate-hike-in-december-sees-more-hikes-to-come-174616572.html

Federal Reserve governor Chris Waller said Wednesday that if the central bank hikes interest rates by a quarter point next month, it will need to signal that the Fed will hike again by that much in January.

Otherwise, the Fed needs to raise rates by a jumbo half percentage point in December.

"People have argued, do you do 50 in December, nothing in January, or 25 in December, 25 in January," Waller said in an interview Wednesday on CNBC. "But when you do 25 now and you're going to do 25 in January, it only affects market pricing if you signal you're going to do it, and you give the markets a credible signal."

Waller said that if you leave the market guessing about a January rate increase, there won't be an effect on the market and, by extension, the economy.

"If you're not going to signal this very clearly to markets you're going in January, my view is then you have to go 50 in December," Waller said.

That sounds a lot like forward guidance, which Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh has sought to eliminate since taking the reins in May. Warsh has said that telegraphing future policy moves distorts market signals and boxes in policymakers.

But Warsh's philosophy hasn't quite taken hold, as other Fed members have continued to offer clues about the path ahead — which has also led to more questions and at times confusion in markets.

Without using the term explicitly, Waller argued Wednesday that the Fed would need to use forward guidance if it doesn't deliver a jumbo hike next month. He said the central bank could signal its next move either in the statement or at Warsh's press conference. He added that quarterly interest rate projections, which will be released in December, would be helpful this time because it's the end of the year and the projections would be for all of next year.

Waller said the Fed needs to be more aggressive because inflation is moving higher after remaining above the central bank's 2% goal for nearly six years.

Waller first hinted at a jumbo hike last week, saying ahead of the Consumer Price Index release, that if inflation came in higher than expected, stronger action would be needed.

The Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October, compared with expectations of 0.3%. "Core" inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, rose 0.3%, compared with expectations of 0.2%.

On Wednesday, Waller said the Fed needs to take back the three rate cuts from last year and may need to do more to get inflation to come back down.
