Jensen Huang and Howard Lutnick will appear publicly before a powerful Senate panel

The flood of Capitol Hill interest in the ongoing scandal at Nvidia (NVDA) could create a traffic jam of high-profile hearings in the weeks ahead, including closely watched public grillings by the Senate's powerful banking panel of two of the figures at the center of ongoing national security questions.

A source in Sen. Tim Scott's office confirmed to Yahoo Finance on Wednesday that both ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick have agreed to appear publicly before the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs, as talks continue over the precise timing.

Scott said on CNBC earlier on Wednesday that he wants to get the testimony done "before Christmas." He had previously requested in a letter that Huang appear on Dec. 3 and Lutnick appear on Dec. 10.

The Commerce Department previously confirmed Lutnick's willingness to appear before the Senate panel, but the confirmation that the testimony will be public — in front of reporters and cameras — sets up what is expected to be one of Washington's most highly anticipated business-world hearings in years as both men face questions about Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy and whether the Trump administration did enough in response to apparent attempts to evade US export controls of advanced semiconductor chips.

The questions will come from Republicans and Democrats, including ranking member Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the committee, who is expected to take the committee's gavel in 2027 when Democrats assume control of the chamber.

Warren has previously accused Huang of "putting our national security at risk" and has promised investigations of her own.

Another Democrat on the panel is Sen. Ruben Gallego of Arizona, who this week posted "slow your roll there" in response to Huang saying he would be honored to return to the company.

Multiple probes into 'a potential breach of our national security'

That hearing is just one on the congressional calendar.

Huang and Nvidia's top lawyer have also agreed to appear in a closed-door hearing before a trio of House committees.

That joint effort by the House's Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China Select committees is set to meet behind closed doors with Huang on Dec. 1. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter will then appear in another closed hearing on Dec. 2.

"We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security," Republican Reps. Brian Mast of Florida, John Moolenaar of Michigan, and Rick Crawford of Arkansas said in a joint statement, adding that both interviews will be transcribed.
