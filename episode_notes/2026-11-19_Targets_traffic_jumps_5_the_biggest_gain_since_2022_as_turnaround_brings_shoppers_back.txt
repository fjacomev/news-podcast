Target's traffic jumps 5%, the biggest gain since 2022, as turnaround brings shoppers back

2026-11-19

Por Brooke DiPalma

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/targets-traffic-jumps-5-the-biggest-gain-since-2022-as-turnaround-brings-shoppers-back-130616166.html

Target (TGT) is plotting its comeback, and it's bringing customers back through the doors.

In the third quarter, the retailer posted a 5% increase in foot traffic, the highest year-over-year jump since January 2022. Net sales grew 7.1% to $27.1 billion, driven by more than 9% growth in beauty and roughly 7% growth in food and beverage.

Michael Fiddelke, who became CEO earlier this year, said this could be a good sign for this holiday shopping season, as consumers continue to spend despite inflation and high gas prices.

"One of the most important things is that we're seeing growth driven by traffic," Fiddelke said on a call with reporters. "It's not just one category. It's not just one activation. It's the cumulative impact of all of what we're doing that has [the] guest picking Target more often. That's encouraging as we head into the holiday season, as we build on this momentum in the years to come."

Same-store sales grew by 5.6%, above estimates of 3.7%, driven by customers visiting more often and also spending slightly more when they did.

Chief merchandising officer Cara Sylvester said the company is investing in new food items, which now account for 10% of overall food sales. Sylvester said Target's private-label and national brands are gaining traction as the retailer leans into deals, such as holiday food and beverage items priced at $5 or less.

Beauty is also seeing double-digit growth across skin care, fragrance, hair, and nail care. As of August 2026, Target officially ended its partnership with Ulta, replacing it with its own in-house concept, Target Beauty Studio, which began rolling out this quarter in September.

There is more work to do in apparel and home, though, Fiddelke said.

"We've made progress but are not satisfied with performance," he said. "It was great to see home turn positive in Q3, but we've said that's gonna be a multiyear journey."

Longtime UBS retail analyst Michael Lasser said those categories are "among the clearest sources of untapped upside," but there needs to be "evidence that assortment, merchandising and execution improvements are beginning to translate into better performance."

Sylvester said Target is making a "slow sequence of progress," working on key categories one by one, such as bedding, bath, and women's clothing and shoes. The retailer has seen success with promotions for a $35 faux-fur blanket and a $45 cashmere sweater, similar to Walmart's approach as of late.

Target increased its fiscal 2026 guidance, now expecting net sales to grow 6%, up from its previous forecast of 5%.
