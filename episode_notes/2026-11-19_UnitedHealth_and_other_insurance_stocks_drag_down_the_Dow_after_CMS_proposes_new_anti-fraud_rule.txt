UnitedHealth and other insurance stocks drag down the Dow after CMS proposes new anti-fraud rule

2026-11-19

Por Grace O'Donnell

https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/article/unitedhealth-and-other-insurance-stocks-drag-down-the-dow-after-cms-proposes-new-anti-fraud-rule-173851810.html

Healthcare stocks fell again on Wednesday after the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) proposed a new rule that would toughen oversight of Medicare, Medicaid, the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP), and the health insurance marketplace.

Sagging shares of managed care providers weighed on the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI). UnitedHealth Group stock (UNH), one of the Dow's largest components with a 4.32% weighting, fell by more than 10%. Humana (HUM) dropped 13%. Cigna (CI) shares declined by 3%. CVS Health (CVS), which is in the process of splitting up its retail and insurance businesses, fell 5%.

The rule, called the Comprehensive Regulations to Uncover Suspicious Healthcare, or CRUSH, would increase audits of insurance providers and allow CMS to recover improper payments. It would also require providers to report changes to enrollment information within 30 days, among other conditions.

In the release shared on Monday, officials pointed to bad actors billing Medicare for nonexistent wheelchairs, enrolling healthy seniors in Medicare-funded hospice care, and signing people up for Marketplace plans without their consent as examples of the kinds of fraud this rule aims to crack down on.

CMS said the rule could save taxpayers nearly $90 billion over the next 10 years. The proposal is open to public comment until Jan. 19, 2027.

However, Oppenheimer analysts said they expect the proposal to face similar legal scrutiny to that of a framework a federal court struck down in 2025. Furthermore, the team noted that the $90 billion cost savings likely reflect CMS recouping repayments rather than broad savings to healthcare programs.

"We believe CMS will be subject to the same legal challenges that it faced in 2025," the analysts wrote. "The substantially larger collection estimate reflects broader auditing, with recoveries concentrated in 2028–2030. This is a proposed rule, so CMS could choose to make adjustments in the final rule. Even if the rule were implemented as is, and it were upheld by the courts, we believe plans would incorporate the prospective cost into bids rather than absorb an unchanged recurring earnings burden."
