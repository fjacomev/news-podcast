Anthropic's IPO ties the fate of the US economy closer to AI

Digital billboards advertise Anthropic on the day of its IPO in Times Square, New York, Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026. (AP Photo/Seth Wenig)

The day finally arrived. Anthropic (

ANT

) began trading on Thursday at $111.50 per share, surging above its $83 IPO price and briefly attaining a market capitalization of $2 trillion before settling into a still-respectable market cap of $1.2 trillion. What took years for the tech giants to achieve,

the frontier AI lab grabbed in an instant

But

the historic IPO is notable not just for its size

, but for what it says — and will say — about the makeup of the stock market and the outsized influence of its largest players.

The top 10 biggest companies make up close to 40% of the S&P 500. A bad day for tech means a bad day for the market. And now that

Anthropic has joined the trillion-dollar ticker club

— though it won’t join the S&P 500 (

^GSPC

) anytime soon — Wall Street's fate is tightly bound to another huge tech company which also happens to be an AI lab without a long track record of financial stability.

The top-heavy nature of the S&P 500 means the benchmark index is less diverse than it looks. Sure, investors are buying into a basket of hundreds of names. But since the tickers with the largest market caps get the highest proportional weights, a handful of names can move the entire index.

That's how you can get such different performances from the equal- weighted S&P 500 (

^SPXEW

) index (up 11% so far this year) versus the standard, market-weighted version (up 6%).

Big Tech's importance in the stock market has attracted praise and concern for years, even before the arrival of ChatGPT.

AI has only deepened

that concentration. The tech giants have adopted the AI boom as their new reason for being. And, in a very literal sense, they have poured huge investments into their own AI operations and a sprawling ecosystem of chipmakers, LLM suppliers, power generators, and infrastructure firms.

Anthropic's IPO adds to tech's significance and amplifies the industry's financial gravity. But the frontier lab is also a (the?) primary supplier of AI tools.

Retail investors are treating Anthropic much differently than SpaceX

Dario Amodei, co-founder and CEO of Anthropic, right, and Daniela Amodei, co-founder and president, left, react during the opening bell ceremony at Nasdaq MarketSite as part of the Anthropic IPO in New York, Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026. (AP Photo/Seth Wenig)

One of the most interesting things we saw on Thursday was how retail investors treated Anthropic.

While it may be the most important IPO in many ways, leading the AI trade where humans actually touch the technology, it had a very different reception than SpaceX.

According to data from Vanda Research, which monitors retail investors, far fewer regular investors were buying stock compared to SpaceX's June IPO, by a pretty big margin.

In its first 30 minutes, SpaceX's $26 million in net retail stock volume beat Anthropic's $19 million. In the first three hours, it was $99 million to $37 million.

But it gets even more interesting!

Those numbers are

net

buying

. In terms of actual volume, which includes

selling

, Anthropic saw $820 million in total volume, compared to SpaceX's $669 million.

In other words, people were way more bullish on SpaceX, but willing to try to "win" the market with Anthropic.

Maybe this makes sense. Elon Musk is not just a multi-CEO but a cult figure whose moonshots are so big they're actually Mars-shots. Anthropic and its CEO Dario Amodei just don't have that brand yet.

Kalshi hired its GTA researcher after 33,000 applications

A billboard for Rockstar Games' Grand Theft Auto VI is seen near Times Square in New York, on November 16, 2026. (CHARLY TRIBALLEAU / AFP via Getty Images) · CHARLY TRIBALLEAU via Getty Images

It was the type of job posting that didn't look real, as we wrote a week ago.

Which is why the job posting began with: "This is a real job."

Kalshi, the prediction market,

was looking for a "GTA VI researcher,"

someone to livestream their playthrough of Take-Two's (

TTWO

) blockbuster title, with an eye for studying the game's economy.

Ahead of

the game's midnight launch Thursday

, Kalshi announced its newest hire, the winner of this wild sweepstakes. He goes by Cliff Savage, a recording artist, streamer, and content creator, who will now add playing GTA VI to his resume.

Kalshi's co-founder and CEO Tarek Mansour said in a post on X that Savage stood out for his work ethic and his passion for the game. Savage has nearly 16,000 monthly listeners on Spotify and more than 2,000 followers on Twitch.

Mansour's announcement also noted that Kalshi received over 33,000 applications, a staggering number that

highlights the intense interest around GTA VI

and the idea of getting paid to play video games and talk about them.

Besides showcasing the interest, the moment was a snapshot of the modern AI-powered labor market. Perhaps even thousands of these applications were AI-generated. But almost certainly, they were AI-screened too, as employees and employers fight fire with fire.

While we may not see these same numbers, 100 applications to a single role isn't uncommon, and getting your resume to a human is becoming a success of its own.
