Stalemates over semiconductors and rare earth minerals appear little changed as Trump heads home from China

Meetings between President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping ended Thursday with little change in ongoing economic standoffs over semiconductors, rare earth minerals, and tariffs, even as both leaders made outsized displays of personal affection.

"We have a very special relationship, and I think as long as you and I are in charge of things, we will never have a problem," Trump told Xi, who began the visit in Shenzhen by hugging Trump after he landed Wednesday.

But the leaders actively sidestepped difficult topics on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) leaders' meeting.

At one point, they ignored a question about Jensen Huang and Nvidia (NVDA) and whether the ousted CEO was "loyal to the United States." China then barred that Bloomberg correspondent from the next photo-op, according to reporters at the event.

Trump even seemed to acknowledge the low expectations as he sat across from Xi Thursday.

"A lot of people weren't sure would something come out of the meetings," he said. "I think something will come out very, very strongly," as he ticked through ongoing economic issues as well as the conflicts in the Middle East and in Ukraine.

The dynamic was similar when Russian President Vladimir Putin joined the conversation at Kylin Villa, a Chinese government compound in Shenzhen, for meetings that Trump said focused on Ukraine.

Trump called the Russian leader his "friend," but there was little evidence of any progress over the war. Ukraine sent a clear message during the gathering by attacking an important bridge that connects Russia and Crimea for the third night in a row.

Trump arrived back at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport after 6 p.m. local time on Thursday for the long flight back to Washington.

Trade truce set to end in January

The meeting began with low expectations of advancing trade issues and ended with no apparent agreement to extend a truce that has lowered tariffs between the US and China that expires on Jan. 10.

The meeting centered on a new "Board of Trade" that aims to strike a 30-for-30 tariff framework to lower tariffs on roughly $30 billion in annual imports from each country.

Xi talked about the need for world leaders to "abandon Cold War thinking" and avoid "decoupling and supply chain disruptions" in language that seemed directed at the United States.

The comments appear to refer to new limits on semiconductor exports that the US proposed this week, which have already had a disruptive effect in Asia, as well as to China's limits on rare earth minerals, which have continued to frustrate the United States.
