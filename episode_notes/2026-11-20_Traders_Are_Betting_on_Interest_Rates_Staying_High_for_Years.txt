Traders Are Betting on Interest Rates Staying High for Years

2026-11-20

Por James Hirai

(Bloomberg) -- Traders are betting that major central banks will keep interest rates largely unchanged for years once they've finished hiking at the end of 2027.

Money markets began 2026 pricing in rate cuts by the Federal Reserve, European Central Bank and Bank of England but had to pivot when the Iran war throttled oil supplies, lifting inflation. Traders flipped to betting on a series of hikes, while also wagering that easing would follow next year.

Those hopes for lower borrowing costs have almost vanished as the conflict drags on and a wave of investment in technology, rearmament and the energy transition boosts competition for funding, fueling higher bond yields. All three central banks are expected to raise rates further in the next 12 months, and then mostly sit tight at least until 2031, based on interest rate futures.

"I call this the yield era," said Bob Stoutjesdijk, investment strategy and portfolio manager at Osmosis Investment Management. "We are living in a world of structurally higher rates of inflation."

Companies went on a borrowing binge to finance investment in artificial intelligence this year, hitting the market with a record flood of bonds and hoovering up hundreds of billions of dollars. There's much more debt to come in 2027, according to JPMorgan Chase & Co., which sees big tech spending, and elevated refinancing needs, driving another record year of US investment-grade bond issuance.

Many Fed policymakers view the AI buildout as a material source of inflation risk, according to minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee's October meeting published this week.

In addition, governments are ratcheting up spending on defense and infrastructure, most notably in Germany, which is set to be the largest gross issuer of debt among euro area peers next year, according to strategists at Commerzbank AG.

Uncertain World

To be sure, oil prices have retreated from a six-month peak in recent weeks, but inflation numbers are still hot, fueling wagers on more rate hikes against a volatile geopolitical backdrop.

"Macro and political uncertainty remain elevated," said Christoph Rieger, head of rates and credit research at Commerzbank.
