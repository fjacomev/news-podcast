7 reasons why tech stocks are getting hammered again

2026-11-21

Por Brian Sozzi

So much for the much-hyped Anthropic (ANT) IPO on Thursday lifting the spirits of tech investors ahead of the holidays.

Lost in all the Anthropic IPO coverage yesterday was the fact that, while the stock finished up nicely on its debut day, the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) fell 2.2%.

The selling pressure in wider tech intensified on Friday, underpinned by several key reasons, pros say.

"This sell-off has been driven by a perfect storm," Yorkville Ives partner Dan Ives wrote.

He listed the catalysts for the latest tech rout: "1) The Blue Tidal Wave win and fears about an anti-AI/anti-datacenter DC, 2) losing the global leader of the AI Revolution Jensen in his black eye moment, 3) China driven regulatory concerns across the rest of semis/memory stocks (e.g. AMD short report yesterday), 4) multiple market clearing events which have just led to more selling, 5) Anthropic IPO being a massive success but marginally reducing liquidity from other tech names, 6) significant fund P&L pain diminishing buying power, and 7) sticky inflation and hawkish Fed speak with concerns around more hikes and constricting financial conditions."

Anthropic is getting roped into the sell-off in day two of public company life. Shares of the model builder dropped 12% in afternoon trading on Friday as investors booked profits after a frenetic first day of trading.

Anthropic made a monster debut on Wall Street Thursday, with shares opening at $111.50 a little before noon ET, a 34% premium to the $83 IPO price.

But the AI company's stock couldn't hold on to its early gains: The stock closed at $97.17, up 17% on the day.

That closing price gave Anthropic a market capitalization of about $1.28 trillion, or roughly $1.75 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

Wall Street is still out there defending tech stocks, especially at valuations not seen in years.

D.A. Davidson analyst Gil Luria said a "clean" earnings report from Nvidia (NVDA) this week and a successful Anthropic IPO "clear the way for AI-driven stocks to start coming back."

Ives added, "We continue to believe a diversified AI approach to the market is the way to own the winners and derivatives of the AI Revolution into 2027: This is a painful tech sell-off that is heavily discounting this 4th Industrial Revolution thesis will take place over the next few years … and that is the wrong move in our view."
