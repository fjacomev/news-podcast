Anthropic's IPO winners and losers, from Ashton Kutcher to Sam Bankman-Fried

2026-11-21

Por Jared Blikre

Ashton Kutcher and Sam Bankman-Fried both bet early on Anthropic. Only one is celebrating its public market debut.

Bankman-Fried's FTX put $500 million into Anthropic (ANT) in 2021 for about 14% of the AI lab. After FTX collapsed, the estate sold the stake in 2024 for about $1.3 billion. Among the buyers was Jane Street, the trading firm where Bankman-Fried got his start.

That stake was worth more than $40 billion at Anthropic's first-day close of $97.17 — even after the stock opened at $111.50, 34% above its $83 IPO price, and gave back part of the pop. Bankman-Fried, who is serving a 25-year sentence, told the New York Times from prison that he tries not to dwell on it. "There's no point in obsessing over regrets," he said. "But it's certainly something that I've spent, despite that, a fair bit of time thinking through."

Kutcher, meanwhile, had a different response: "Kind of cool," the actor said of the IPO on X. Sound Ventures, the firm he co-founded with Madonna's manager Guy Oseary, first backed Anthropic in 2023. Its $435 million turned into about $6 billion by the close, with some outlets reporting that the debut could make Kutcher a billionaire, despite his recent exit from the firm. NBA star Tristan Thompson is another celebrity backer, and he isn't selling.

Everyone else got rich too

Anthropic's seven co-founders were worth about $163 billion combined at the close, or more than $23 billion apiece, by Yahoo Finance calculations. They were the only insiders selling in the IPO, at about $280 million each.

The seven have pledged to give away 80% of their stock. That's about $130 billion at the close, more than the Gates Foundation's entire $92 billion endowment. The filing calls the pledges "voluntary, revocable," and the seven keep 50.1% of the vote either way.

Employees did fine as well. Their options and restricted stock were worth about $270 billion at the close. The average option costs just $2.91 a share to exercise, on a stock that closed at $97.17.

Then there's Big Tech

Amazon (AMZN) has put in $18 billion, and its roughly 15% stake was worth $190 billion at the close. Alphabet (GOOG, GOOGL) owns about 14%, worth about $175 billion. Nvidia (NVDA) had the quickest payday, turning a $10 billion buy at the IPO price into a gain of about $1.7 billion by the close.

Anyone who chased the open came up short, down about 13% by the close. Pricing 980 million shares at $83 left nearly $14 billion on the table, just shy of SpaceX's record $14.4 billion in University of Florida professor Jay Ritter's data. Its bankers didn't, with Goldman Sachs (GS) and Morgan Stanley (MS) set to collect about $113 million each.
