Lisa Cook warns the Fed could get behind the curve if it doesn't raise rates now

2026-11-23

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/lisa-cook-warns-the-fed-could-get-behind-the-curve-if-it-doesnt-raise-rates-now-171832457.html

Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook said Friday that the central bank risks getting behind the curve in bringing inflation down if it doesn't raise interest rates now.

"Inflation has been above target for more than five and a half years, and every additional day poses greater risk that price- and wage-setters will adjust their inflation expectations upward," Cook said in a speech in Santiago, Chile. "Waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve."

Cook dissented in October for the first time against the Fed's decision to hold rates steady.

On Friday, she said that urgency, along with accommodative financial conditions, led her to dissent last month. Cook said financial conditions are too accommodative at a time when inflation is stubbornly too high with no signs of coming down. She doesn't think rates are curtailing the artificial intelligence build-out, and she believes stock prices continue to support consumer spending. Holding rates steady again, Cook warned, would increase inflation pressure.

Cook pointed to inflation reports since the Fed's last policy meeting on Oct. 28, which show prices are rising briskly in a broad range of categories — including airfare, appliances, furniture, hotels, and restaurants. She estimates that the Fed's favored inflation measure — the Personal Consumption Expenditures index — rose 3.8% in October. Stripping out volatile food and energy prices, she estimates PCE rose an estimated 3.2% on a "core" basis.

Cook said the core measure shows that, while energy prices have risen due to the conflict in the Middle East, the sources of price pressure are varied.

She stopped short of providing any specific outlook on what the Fed should do at its meeting in December. Fed Governor Chris Waller has raised the prospect of raising by a jumbo 50 basis point hike.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC Friday that the Fed should be forward-looking, given that energy prices are dropping.
