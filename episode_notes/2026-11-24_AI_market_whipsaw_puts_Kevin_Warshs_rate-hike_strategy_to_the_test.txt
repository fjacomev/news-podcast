AI market whipsaw puts Kevin Warsh's rate-hike strategy to the test

2026-11-24

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/ai-market-whipsaw-puts-kevin-warshs-rate-hike-strategy-to-the-test-100000273.html

When stocks tanked last week, Wall Street briefly bet the Federal Reserve would ease up on interest rate hikes. Now Monday's massive rebound — sparked by Jensen Huang's reinstatement at Nvidia — has revived bets on at least two more interest rate hikes.

Yet with AI stocks still off their highs, the sector's volatility is emerging as a critical test for Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh and the central bank's path for rate hikes.

"AI stress is not mostly due to the Fed. But it is not a coincidence it is occurring when the Fed is hiking," said Krishna Guha, head of central banking and the economy for Evercore ISI. "Talk of 50-basis-point hikes or faster tightening adds to stress. Fed Chair Warsh not talking is a problem. It leaves the market guessing: Is he a hardcore hawk or a market-sensitive hawk? The gap is filled by others whose views can lag the leadership."

"This is a serious test for Warsh," Guha added.

With Warsh silent during the market's recent swings, investors are parsing both the divided commentary from his colleagues and the clues he left behind at his late-October press conference.

Last week, Fed officials offered competing visions for how aggressively to move. Fed governor Chris Waller said that if the central bank hikes by a quarter point next month, it will need to signal a follow-up hike of the same size in January — or else deliver a jumbo half-percentage-point increase in December. Fed governor Lisa Cook echoed that urgency on Friday, right as the market hit its nadir. Arguing that financial conditions remain too accommodative while inflation stays stubbornly high, she warned the central bank risks falling behind the curve if it doesn't hike now.

New York Fed president John Williams, however, pushed back against calls for supersize rate hikes, saying last week that he doesn't think the situation calls for "dramatic action."

For his part, Warsh staked out a middle-ground framework following the Fed's decision to hold rates steady on Oct. 28 — three-and-a-half weeks before the tech rout. While noting that overall financial and credit conditions had tightened since September's rate hike, he stopped short of calling them "tight." Crucially, he also signaled he is watching the AI sector closely, noting that credit spreads had widened for certain assets and acknowledging that interest rates are not "somehow divorced" from the AI investment surge.

Now, Wall Street is divided over whether Warsh's focus on financial conditions will restrain the central bank, or if his inflation target will force him to keep hiking into a fragile market.
