Fed's Williams: Fed must be forward-looking to bring inflation down

2026-11-24

Por Jennifer Schonberger

https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/feds-williams-fed-must-be-forward-looking-to-bring-inflation-down-212531171.html

New York Federal Reserve president John Williams said Monday that the central bank needs to be "forward-looking" on inflation and that there's still work to do to bring down prices.

"Monetary policy works with a lag of one to two years for its maximal effect on the economy," Williams said in a speech in New York. "Therefore, it is essential that we remain forward-looking and not overly focused on inflation's path in the past."

Williams noted that it's the Fed's job to make sure that higher energy prices, tariffs, and price increases from AI do not lead to broader or persistent inflation, saying that the Fed "must take actions over time" that ensure inflation gets back to the central bank's 2% goal in a timely manner.

"There is still work to do to get that done," he said.

Williams said he expects that data since the Fed's October policy meeting will show inflation has continued to run too high.

That Williams said the Fed needs to be more forward-looking does not match the approach the Fed has taken in recent years. The central bank has tended to be data-dependent, which is inherently more backward-looking. Williams didn't suggest that the Fed not consult data, but rather that any actions taken today will take a year or two to be implemented. With energy prices coming down, a lot could change.

Williams previously threw cold water on the prospect of a jumbo rate hike. When asked whether that latest inflation data calls for a supersize rate hike soon, Williams said, "I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action."

Williams estimates a rate of 3.5% this year, but he sees it slowing to just above 2% next year as the effects of tariffs move further away, energy prices normalize, and the demand and supply of AI-related goods move back toward better balance.

While much of the overall strength in the economy can be traced to the AI-related boom and consumer spending, Williams said he sees a "two-track" economy where important parts are strong, but growth is at or below its trend rate.

He noted that sectors like residential investment and non-AI-related structures investment have weakened further over the past several quarters. This weakness also showed up in recent surveys the New York Fed conducted with businesses in the New York area, which report that while manufacturing is strong, business activity in the services sector has fallen sharply.

"This leaves us at an unusual macroeconomic juncture," Williams said. "The economic data are strong. The labor market is stable. And this strength is not creating macroeconomic imbalances in the labor market. Despite this overall balance, high inflation is proving persistent."
