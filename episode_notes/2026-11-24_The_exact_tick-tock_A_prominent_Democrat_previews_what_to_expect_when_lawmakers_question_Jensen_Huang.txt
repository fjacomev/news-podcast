'The exact tick-tock': A prominent Democrat previews what to expect when lawmakers question Jensen Huang

Democratic Sen. Ruben Gallego of Arizona said to expect "a stronger hearing than normal" when lawmakers have a chance to grill now-reinstated Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang next week.

"What I want to know is the exact tick-tock of how this all occurred," Gallego said, listing questions such as when Huang learned about suspicions of his paramour's involvement with Chinese intelligence, and whether he purposely helped her company evade a ban on semiconductor exports, as well as President Trump's involvement.

"I think it does bring sharper questions into focus," he added.

Gallego spoke to Yahoo Finance on Monday shortly after the Senate Banking Committee, where Gallego sits as a member, confirmed that Jensen's testimony will go forward on Dec. 3.

Gallego's comments underscore the battle Huang will face in Washington in the months ahead, despite having Trump's backing.

Many lawmakers, even in Trump's own party, are not fully on board with Huang's reinstatement. Hard questions in the hearing a week from Thursday are likely to come from both sides of the aisle.

Another member of the panel, Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts of Nebraska, posted on X Monday, listing issues dogging Huang, such as his reported multiple affairs and lack of "candor" with his own board of directors.

"His behavior does not support reinstatement," Ricketts wrote.

'Democrats will wield their full power'

Gallego's comments were part of a flurry from Democrats who quickly promised aggressive oversight when they take power in January.

"Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives," Chuck Schumer, the likely Senate majority leader, wrote on X.

"Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Democratic Sen. Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate's banking panel and in line to take over in 2027, said in a statement in which she called Huang's reinstatement "a reckless decision."

Gallego — who is mentioned as a possible 2028 presidential candidate — said he also wants to hear from members of Nvidia's board of directors, as well as officials at the Commerce Department who ran the semiconductor export control program, whom he called "the executioners of this."

He said he is keenly interested in whether the president or members of his team, including Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, profited from Nvidia's gyrating stock or previous loopholes in semiconductor exports.
