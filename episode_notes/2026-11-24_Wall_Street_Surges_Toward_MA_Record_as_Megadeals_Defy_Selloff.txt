(Bloomberg) -- Wall Street is taking a big step toward a record year for mergers and acquisitions as some of the world's largest companies defy a tech-induced selloff in global stocks to pursue major takeovers.

Most Read from Bloomberg

CrowdStrike Holdings Inc. (CRWD) on Monday confirmed Bloomberg News reporting that it will acquire cybersecurity provider Zscaler Inc. (ZS) The near $44 billion transaction highlights the shift toward a smaller number of companies protecting more of the world's computers and networks.

It's the second megadeal announced in the global technology industry this month, following Marvell Technology Inc.'s planned purchase of Lumentum Holdings Inc. for around $115 billion including debt, and comes as dealmakers work to recover from a recent slump in activity brought on in part by concerns about a new rate-hiking cycle.

Elsewhere on Monday, Texas Pacific Land Corp. struck a roughly $3 billion purchase of EagleRock Land; Var Energi ASA agreed to acquire ConocoPhillips assets in Norway and the UK for about $2.5 billion including debt; and ABB Ltd. said it will buy privately held German cooling systems company Ziehl-Abegg SE for an equity value of $2.2 billion.

The value of deals announced globally this year now sits at around $4.6 trillion, according to data compiled by Bloomberg. That's about $700 billion shy of a new annual record. Dealmakers' chances of passing that milestone could hinge on a clutch of multibillion-dollar transactions already being discussed across of a range of major industries.

In financial services, private equity firm GTCR is pursuing a takeover of Global Payments Inc. in what, at around $50 billion, would be one of the biggest-ever leveraged buyouts. And in healthcare, Sanofi SA is weighing an acquisition of US drugmaker Insmed Inc. in what could end up being the largest tie-up in the global biopharma sector for three years. And Clariant AG and CD&R are in talks to acquire chemicals maker Ashland Inc.

Wall Street advisers say that, following the midterm elections in the US, companies are operating under a renewed sense of urgency to chase transformative takeovers while M&A-friendly regulators remain in place under President Donald Trump.
