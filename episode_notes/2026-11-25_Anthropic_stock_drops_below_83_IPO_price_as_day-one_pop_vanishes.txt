Anthropic stock drops below $83 IPO price as day-one pop vanishes

2026-11-25

Por Jared Blikre

Anthropic's (ANT) first trade was worth $12 billion. Three sessions later, anyone who bought it was down more than 30%.

Anthropic stock slipped below its $83 IPO price Tuesday in its fourth session as a public company. By late morning, it had dropped below $76. The stock opened at $111.50 on Nov. 19 and closed its first day at $97.17. On Friday, it came within a nickel of the line. Minutes after Tuesday's open, a share cost less than $80, about the price of "Grand Theft Auto VI," the video game that launched the same day Anthropic did.

The sellers have plenty to work with. Insiders can sell up to 316 million shares, more than a quarter as many as the IPO sold, according to Anthropic's IPO filing.

Anthropic is still bid among individual investors, who bought about $100 million more shares than they sold on both Friday and Monday, making it their second-most-bought stock, according to Vanda Research.

Passive index funds are next in line. Anthropic could join the Nasdaq-100 on a fast track as soon as Dec. 21, which would force index-tracking funds, such as the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ), to buy, according to a Themes ETFs analysis.

Most broken IPO stocks stay broken

Breaking the IPO price is a bad start, not a verdict. What came next depended on how far each stock fell.

The shallow dips healed the fastest. Visa (V), Arm (ARM), and Alibaba (BABA) each bottomed within about three months, less than 20% below their IPO prices, and doubled those prices within two years.

The broader record is tougher

About a quarter of the 2,734 US IPOs from 2001 through 2024 closed their first day below the offer price, and two-thirds of those were lower three years later, according to data from University of Florida professor Jay Ritter. Anthropic held its line longer than that group but not by much.

As Creative Planning's Charlie Bilello put it on X, "the best time for Wall Street to sell IPOs to the public has been the worst time for the public to buy."
