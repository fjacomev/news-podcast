Home prices rose 2.6% in September even as mortgage rates moved higher

Home prices rose again in September, though the gains continue to trail inflation.

The S&P Cotality Case-Shiller 20-City Composite Home Price Index, which measures home prices in 20 of the nation's largest metropolitan areas, increased 2.6% from a year earlier, according to data released Tuesday.

The national index, which covers a broader range of cities, increased 2.1%. The latest jump in prices came even as mortgage rates rose back above 7% in September, chilling sales activity during an already seasonally slower period for the housing market.

"Nominal home prices remain resilient, but inflation is once again widening the gap between nominal and real housing returns," Rebecca Kaufman, associate director of commodities at S&P Dow Jones Indices, said in a statement.

Prices across a broad range of goods and services rose 3.7% in September, largely due to higher oil prices.

And housing performance continues to vary widely by metropolitan area. Prices in Chicago are up nearly 7% annually, followed by New York with a 5.8% gain and Miami with a 4.6% increase. But home prices are down 1.9% in Seattle and roughly 1% in Denver and Dallas.

Claire Boston is a senior reporter for Yahoo Finance covering housing, mortgages, and home insurance.
