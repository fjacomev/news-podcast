Huang faces 3 camps of GOP skeptics ahead of congressional grilling

2026-11-25

Por Ben Werschkul

https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/huang-faces-3-camps-of-gop-skeptics-ahead-of-congressional-grilling-164400869.html

President Trump's full-throated support for Jensen Huang and his reinstatement atop Nvidia (NVDA) has not been matched by Republican lawmakers, who say they intend to keep the pressure on the chipmaker.

The objections broadly fall into three categories. A handful of Republicans say it was a mistake to reinstate Huang as CEO. Another group says they have serious concerns and feel that US national security has been compromised. And a third group says simply that they have unanswered questions about the whole affair.

According to a Yahoo Finance tally, a majority of the 13 Republican senators whom Huang will face next week on the Senate Banking Committee have offered at least a promise of tough questions, and two members have even said Huang should not be in the CEO chair.

North Dakota Republican Kevin Cramer recently posted that the legal cloud was "risk enough to not rehire Huang," while Nebraska's Pete Ricketts said his "behavior does not support reinstatement." Both will have time to question Huang.

The hearing will be overseen by Chair Tim Scott of South Carolina, who said in his own statement that national security will be "central to the committee's oversight."

Across Congress, there are now well over a dozen GOP lawmakers who have said publicly that they aren't convinced (as Trump claims) that national security hasn't been compromised. That's in addition to a wide array of figures across the party, from Newt Gingrich to Mitt Romney to Laura Loomer.

Democrats, meanwhile, are all but universal in their condemnation. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the committee, is promising investigations now and into 2027. Arizona Sen. Ruben Gallego, another prominent Democrat on the banking panel, told Yahoo Finance this week he wants "the exact tick-tock" of what Huang knew and when he knew it.

Huang is facing a gauntlet of hearings after Thanksgiving. He is first scheduled to answer questions from House members on Dec. 1 behind closed doors. A trio of Republican leaders atop that effort have accused Nvidia of allowing "mass smuggling."

He will then appear before senators — and cameras — on Thursday in a public hearing of the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs.

Many market observers say the road ahead will be a bumpy one not just for him and his company — but for the semiconductor sector as a whole.

"Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO may have provided near-term relief for the market, but we believe it will intensify congressional scrutiny of the company, the AI industry, and increases the probability of bipartisan legislation that restricts the sale of advanced technology to China," Ed Mills of Raymond James wrote in a note to clients.
