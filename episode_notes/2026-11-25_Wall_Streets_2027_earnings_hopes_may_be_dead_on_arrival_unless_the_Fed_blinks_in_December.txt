Wall Street's 2027 earnings hopes may be dead on arrival unless the Fed blinks in December

2026-11-25

Por Jared Blikre

Wall Street spent Monday buying back the AI trade. The bill for next year's AI spending still comes due in the bond market.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC) jumped nearly 5% Monday, its best day since April 2025, as Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia (NVDA) sparked a $3 trillion comeback. Stocks were mixed Tuesday, with chip stocks leading tech lower again.

But Monday's rescue came from the White House, not the Fed. Nvidia is "systemic at this point," Naveen Rao, CEO of Unconventional AI, said on Deirdre Bosa Live. "It was a little bit like the banking crisis."

A president can help put a CEO back in charge. He can't make money cheaper. Only the Fed can, and Polymarket traders see a better-than-90% probability of another rate hike on Dec. 9.

Alfonso Peccatiello, founder of the Macro Compass, sees today as "a much more similar setup to 2018 than it was in 2000 or 2007." The big difference is inflation.

By Peccatiello's math, about half of the roughly 17% earnings growth Wall Street expects next year assumes AI spending jumps 48% to more than $1 trillion.

That money has to be borrowed or raised, like Meta's (META) nearly $100 billion stock sale. Companies "need low market volatility and risk appetite to keep issuing to finance their spending," Peccatiello wrote.

Telecom tried this in 2001

He points to telecom, whose spending "fell by 30-40% compared to expectations despite long-term contracts because the financing chain broke down" in 2001 and 2002.

Today's contracts may not hold up, either. Even after Monday's rally, Madhu Namburi of General Catalyst warned on Deirdre Bosa Live that rising rates are squeezing neoclouds, the AI cloud renters Nvidia helps finance. One he wouldn't name "had two massive contracts. One of the contracts just got canceled."

The last time the Fed blinked

In 2018, the Fed hiked into a nearly 20% drop, then signaled a pause about two weeks later. Stocks were back at a record by April.

This time, the Fed's hands are tied. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, is running at 3.1% annually versus 2% back then. "The FOMC bar to turn dovish is quite high," Peccatiello wrote.

His script is a bounce, then more pain. "Bears get frustrated, bulls are flocking back in and mocking everyone for not buying the dips," he wrote in the early morning hours of Monday's rally. He expects the S&P 500 to eventually drop toward 6,500.

The bond market may not wait

Credit spreads, the extra yield investors demand to lend to companies, "could have another 100-200 bps (!) to go," Peccatiello wrote. In a BofA Global Research survey, 52% of fund managers expect a hyperscaler to cut spending within six months, and 38% named AI hyperscaler bonds the likeliest source of a credit event.
